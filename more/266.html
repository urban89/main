Habitat Description

Found exclusively in the waters surrounding Mount Rinjani in Lombok, Indonesia, this species inhabits small, clear mountain streams with moderate flow. The habitat is characterized by a rocky substrate and patches of dense vegetation. The water is soft to moderately hard and slightly acidic to neutral, with temperatures typically ranging from 22°C to 27°C (72°F to 81°F).

Scientific Facts

Part of the Cyprinidae family, these fish are relatively small, usually reaching about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches) in length. As of 2024, the species is listed as Least Concern, but habitat degradation due to agricultural runoff and land use changes poses a potential threat. They are egg scatterers that show no parental care, with eggs hatching within 24 to 48 hours.

Life Style Summary

Active and social, these fish prefer to live in groups of six or more. They tend to swim in the middle to upper levels of the water column, constantly exploring their environment. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet that includes high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as mosquito larvae and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

During breeding season, males display a unique behavior of rapidly vibrating their bodies to attract females, creating small ripples on the water surface. This unusual courtship display is believed to help synchronize spawning, ensuring that eggs and sperm are released simultaneously for better fertilization success.
