Habitat Description

The Clown Killifish (Epiplatys annulatus) is native to the shallow, slow-moving freshwater habitats of West Africa, particularly in countries like Liberia, Sierra Leone, and Guinea. They are commonly found in small streams, swamps, and densely vegetated areas with soft, slightly acidic water. The natural habitat is characterized by shaded conditions, with overhanging vegetation that limits light penetration, creating a calm and subdued environment.

Scientific Facts

Clown Killifish belong to the family Nothobranchiidae and are among the smallest of the killifish species, reaching a maximum size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They are known for their distinctive coloration, with alternating black and yellow bands on their body and brightly colored red and blue fins. Epiplatys annulatus is not currently considered endangered, but its populations can be impacted by habitat degradation and pollution. This species lays eggs among fine-leaved plants, and the fry hatch in about 10 to 14 days.

Life Style Summary

Clown Killifish are peaceful and prefer to live in small groups or pairs. They are surface-dwellers and are often seen swimming near the top of the water column, where they feed on small insects, larvae, and zooplankton. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality micro-pellets, flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, and mosquito larvae. They do best in a tank with gentle water flow, plenty of floating plants, and a temperature range of 22-26°C (72-79°F).

Interesting Fact

Clown Killifish are sometimes referred to as "Banded Panchax" due to their unique color pattern. They are jumpers by nature, and it is recommended to keep a tight-fitting lid on their aquarium to prevent them from escaping. Their striking appearance and small size make them a popular choice for nano aquariums.
