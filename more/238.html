Habitat Description

Symphysodon aequifasciatus, commonly known as the Discus, inhabits the slow-moving, warm waters of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil and Peru. These waters are typically soft and acidic, with abundant submerged vegetation and root systems. Discus fish thrive in shaded areas with minimal light penetration, surrounded by dense underwater plants.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cichlidae, Discus can grow up to 20 cm (8 inches) in length. Their rounded, disc-shaped body gives them their common name. First described in 1904, this species is renowned for its vibrant, patterned coloration. As of 2024, Discus are not considered endangered, though habitat loss and overfishing for the aquarium trade are ongoing concerns.

Life Style Summary

These are social fish, preferring to live in groups. They are generally peaceful but can become territorial during breeding. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, pellets, and live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp. They prefer warm water temperatures between 26-30°C (79-86°F) and require excellent water quality to stay healthy.

Interesting Fact

Discus fish exhibit an unusual form of parental care. Both parents secrete a special mucus on their skin that the fry feed on during the early stages of life, ensuring the young receive essential nutrients.
