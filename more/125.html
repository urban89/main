Habitat Description

The Ornate Tetra (Hyphessobrycon bentosi) is native to the slow-moving waters of the Amazon River basin in South America, particularly in Brazil and Peru. It inhabits clear and blackwater streams with abundant plant cover, fallen leaves, and submerged roots. These waters are typically soft, slightly acidic to neutral, with a substrate composed of fine sand or mud, providing a rich environment for small invertebrates and organic matter.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Characidae and is characterized by its elongated body and unique fin shapes, often displaying a reddish or pink hue. They usually reach a maximum size of about 5 cm (2 inches). Ornate Tetras are egg scatterers, showing no parental care for their young. The eggs typically hatch in 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, meaning their populations are stable in their natural habitats without immediate threats.

Life Style Summary

Ornate Tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers that prefer the middle to upper levels of the water column. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, crustaceans, and organic detritus. In aquariums, they do well with a diet that includes high-quality flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The name "bentosi" is derived from the species' original collector, J. Bentos, who first brought it to the attention of ichthyologists. Unlike many other tetras, the Ornate Tetra exhibits a unique courtship behavior where males display their extended dorsal fins to attract females, creating a dynamic and visually captivating display during mating rituals.
