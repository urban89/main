Habitat Description

The Neon Tetra (Paracheirodon innesi) originates from the clear, slow-moving waters of the Amazon basin, particularly in the tributaries of Brazil, Colombia, and Peru. These areas are characterized by soft, slightly acidic water and dense vegetation. The natural habitat is shaded by thick rainforest canopies, allowing minimal light to penetrate, creating a dimly lit environment with a soft, diffused light that enhances the vibrant colors of the fish.

Scientific Facts

Neon Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are among the most popular species in the aquarium trade. They typically grow to about 3.5 cm (1.5 inches) in length. Neon Tetras are known for their iridescent blue line that runs along each side of their body, complemented by a red stripe from the middle to the tail. They are egg scatterers, and like many tetras, they do not exhibit parental care. The species is not currently endangered, but like many Amazonian species, it is affected by habitat loss due to deforestation and pollution.

Life Style Summary

Neon Tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and usually occupy the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and organic matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. Neon Tetras thrive in water temperatures between 20-26°C (68-79°F) and prefer a well-planted aquarium with soft, slightly acidic water.

Interesting Fact

The popularity of Neon Tetras surged after their discovery in the 1930s, captivating aquarists with their brilliant colors that seem to glow under low light conditions. This visual effect, though not true bioluminescence, is due to how light reflects off their unique scales, making them one of the most visually striking fish in the aquarium hobby.
