Habitat Description

The Neon Tetra (Paracheirodon innesi) originates from the clear, slow-moving waters of the Amazon basin in South America, particularly in the tributaries of Brazil, Colombia, and Peru. They are commonly found in densely vegetated areas with soft, slightly acidic water. The natural habitat is shaded by thick rainforest canopies, where light penetration is minimal, creating a subdued environment.

Scientific Facts

Neon Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are one of the most popular species in the aquarium trade. They are small, schooling fish, typically growing to about 1.5 inches in length. The species is recognized by its striking iridescent blue line running along each side of the body, complemented by a bright red stripe from the middle of the body to the tail. Neon Tetras are egg scatterers and exhibit no parental care, with the eggs hatching in about 24 hours.

Life Style Summary

Neon Tetras are peaceful and social fish, preferring to live in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and spend most of their time in the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and organic matter. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Despite their delicate appearance, Neon Tetras are quite hardy and can adapt to a range of water conditions. Their popularity skyrocketed after their discovery in the 1930s, when aquarists were captivated by their vibrant colors, which seemed to glow even under low light. This bioluminescent effect, though not true bioluminescence, is a result of how light reflects off their unique scales, making them a stunning addition to any aquarium.
