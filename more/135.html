Habitat Description

The X-ray Tetra (Pristella maxillaris) inhabits the coastal rivers and streams of South America, particularly in the Amazon and Orinoco river basins. These environments are characterized by soft, slightly acidic to neutral water with a mixture of sandy and muddy substrates. The natural habitat often has dense vegetation and submerged roots, providing ample cover and food sources.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Characidae and is named for its translucent body, which allows its internal organs and backbone to be partially visible, resembling an X-ray image. X-ray Tetras reach an average length of about 5 cm (2 inches). They are egg scatterers with no parental care, and their eggs hatch within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, they are classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in the wild.

Life Style Summary

These tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that do best in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers, preferring the middle levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant debris. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

X-ray Tetras have a unique ability to tolerate a wide range of water conditions, including brackish environments, due to a specialized organ called the "salt gland." This adaptation allows them to survive in both freshwater and slightly salty waters, making them one of the most versatile tetra species in the aquarium trade.
