Habitat Description

The Bloodfin Tetra (Aphyocharax anisitsi) is found in the freshwater rivers and streams of South America, particularly in Argentina, Paraguay, and Brazil. These fish inhabit clear, shallow waters with moderate currents and plenty of vegetation. The environment usually has a sandy or muddy substrate with scattered rocks and leaf litter, providing plenty of hiding spots and a slightly acidic to neutral pH.

Scientific Facts

A member of the Characidae family, this species is recognized by its distinctive red fins and silver body. Bloodfin Tetras grow up to 5 cm (2 inches) in length and are known for their schooling behavior, providing a dynamic display in aquariums. They are listed as "Least Concern" regarding conservation status, with stable populations in the wild (2024). The species is an egg scatterer with no parental care, and the eggs typically hatch within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

Bloodfin Tetras are hardy and adaptable, thriving in groups of six or more. They prefer swimming in the middle to upper levels of the aquarium and benefit from a planted environment that mimics their natural habitat. Their diet in the wild consists of small insects, larvae, and plant debris. In captivity, they readily accept flake foods, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Bloodfin Tetras have the remarkable ability to change the intensity of the red color in their fins depending on their health and stress levels. When they are healthy and in a well-maintained environment, their fins display a vivid red hue. However, under stress or poor water conditions, the color can fade significantly, making their fins an indicator of their overall well-being.
