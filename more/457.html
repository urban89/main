Habitat Description

The Puntigrus tetrazona is native to the streams and tributaries of Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaysia, Borneo, and Sumatra. It is commonly found in clear to turbid water bodies with a moderate to fast flow. These environments are often rich in aquatic vegetation, submerged roots, and leaf litter, providing ample hiding spaces and feeding grounds. The water is usually soft and slightly acidic to neutral, with temperatures ranging from 23-26°C (73-79°F).

Scientific Facts

Tiger Barbs belong to the family Cyprinidae and typically grow to about 7 cm (2.8 inches) in length. Recognized for their bold black stripes against a golden or orange body, they are a popular species in the aquarium trade. As of 2024, this species is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to its wide distribution and stable populations in the wild. They are egg scatterers with no parental care, and their eggs hatch within 24 to 36 hours after spawning.

Life Style Summary

Tiger Barbs are highly active and social fish that thrive in groups of at least six or more. They tend to occupy the middle levels of the aquarium and are known for their playful, sometimes fin-nipping behavior. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they require a varied diet, including high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

This species exhibits hierarchical schooling behavior, where the most dominant fish leads the group. When kept in larger groups, their fin-nipping tendency is reduced, as they tend to focus on establishing hierarchy within their own school rather than bothering other fish species.
