Habitat Description

The Royal Tetra (Inpaichthys kerri) is native to the freshwater rivers and streams of the Brazilian Amazon, particularly in the Rio Aripuanã basin. These fish are commonly found in slow-moving or still waters with a soft, sandy or muddy substrate. Their natural habitat is characterized by dense vegetation and dim lighting due to the thick canopy of the rainforest, creating a slightly acidic environment with low mineral content.

Scientific Facts

Royal Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are known for their striking coloration, with iridescent blue bodies and a distinct royal purple hue on their fins. They typically grow to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. Inpaichthys kerri is currently not considered an endangered species, although its population in the wild may be affected by deforestation and habitat degradation in the Amazon region. The species is recognized for its schooling behavior and is an egg scatterer, laying eggs among plants with no parental care.

Life Style Summary

Royal Tetras are peaceful, social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and prefer to occupy the middle levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, larvae, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, and bloodworms. A tank with plenty of plants and subdued lighting is ideal for this species.

Interesting Fact

The Royal Tetra is known for its unique coloration, which changes depending on the lighting conditions. Under bright light, their blue and purple hues become more intense, making them a visually striking addition to any aquarium. This species was named after the Brazilian ichthyologist Luiz Kerri, who first described the species in the 1980s.
