Habitat Description

The Hemigrammus hyanuary inhabits the calm, shallow waters of the Amazon River basin, particularly around the region of Lake Hyanuary in Brazil, from which it derives its name. These areas are characterized by dense vegetation and soft, slightly acidic water, with sandy or muddy substrates. The environment is shaded by overhanging trees, creating a low-light habitat that is rich in organic material and biofilm.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Characidae and typically grows to about 5 cm (2 inches) in length. It is identified by its slender body, a silvery sheen, and a distinctive dark band running from the gill cover to the base of the tail. As of the latest assessment (2024), it is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, with stable populations in its natural habitat. The January Tetra is an egg scatterer, with eggs hatching within 24 to 36 hours after spawning, and it shows no parental care.

Life Style Summary

These fish are social and thrive in schools of six or more. They are peaceful and active, often found swimming in the middle to upper levels of their habitat. In the wild, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant debris. In an aquarium setting, they do well on a diet of flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. They prefer well-planted tanks that mimic their natural environment.

Interesting Fact

The January Tetra has a unique ability to change its color intensity depending on mood, light, and environmental conditions. This color change is used for communication within the school and can indicate stress, mating readiness, or territory establishment.
