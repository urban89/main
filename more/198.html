Habitat Description

The Malawi Barracuda (Rhamphochromis cf. macrophthalmus) is native to the open waters of Lake Malawi, where it prefers deep and clear environments. This species is typically found at depths ranging from 20 to 100 meters, where it preys on smaller fish. The water in these regions is clear with temperatures between 23-28°C (73-82°F) and a pH of 7.8 to 8.6.

Scientific Facts

This predatory cichlid belongs to the family Cichlidae and can reach up to 30 cm (12 inches) in length. Known for its elongated body and large mouth, it is adapted to chase and capture fast-moving prey in open water. As of 2024, Rhamphochromis cf. macrophthalmus is not considered endangered, but overfishing and changes in the lake's ecosystem could threaten its population in the future.

Life Style Summary

The Malawi Barracuda is a solitary hunter, roaming the open waters in search of smaller fish. In aquariums, it requires a large tank with plenty of open space to swim and a high-protein diet consisting of live or frozen fish and high-quality carnivorous pellets. Due to its predatory nature, it should be kept with other large and robust species or in a species-specific setup to avoid conflicts.

Interesting Fact

This species is known for its remarkable speed and agility, which allow it to ambush and capture prey in the open water. Its streamlined body and powerful tail fin make it one of the most efficient hunters in Lake Malawi, capable of reaching impressive speeds when pursuing its prey.
