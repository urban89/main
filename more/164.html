Habitat Description

The Red Tail Barracuda (Acestrorhynchus falcatus) is native to the freshwater rivers and streams of the Amazon and Orinoco basins in South America. It prefers clear, slow-moving waters with moderate currents and is commonly found in open areas of the water column, where it can actively hunt for prey. The habitat is usually lined with submerged vegetation and woody debris, providing both cover and ambush points.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Acestrorhynchidae, known for their torpedo-shaped bodies and sharp teeth. The Red Tail Barracuda can grow up to 30 cm (12 inches) in length. It is a fast, aggressive predator, feeding mainly on smaller fish. As of now, there are no conservation concerns, and the species is not considered endangered in the wild (2024).

Life Style Summary

Red Tail Barracudas are solitary hunters, preferring to patrol the mid-to-upper levels of the water column. Their primary hunting strategy involves rapid bursts of speed to ambush prey. In captivity, they require a large aquarium with plenty of open space for swimming and a diet consisting of live or frozen fish, though they may also accept meaty foods like shrimp or krill.

Interesting Fact

Despite their common name, Red Tail Barracudas are not closely related to the marine barracuda species. They get their name from their similar appearance and predatory nature. One interesting feature of this species is their ability to leap out of the water to capture prey or escape threats, making them a challenging species to keep in open-top aquariums without proper lids.
