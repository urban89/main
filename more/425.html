Habitat Description

The Potamotrygon leopoldi, commonly known as the Black Devil Stingray, is native to the Xingu River basin in Brazil. This freshwater species is found in clear, fast-flowing rivers with sandy and rocky substrates. The environment is characterized by moderate to strong currents and a temperature range of 24-30°C (75-86°F), with slightly acidic to neutral pH levels. The stingray often inhabits areas with submerged wood and rocks, which provide cover and hunting grounds.

Scientific Facts

Black Devil Stingrays belong to the family Potamotrygonidae, which comprises all freshwater stingrays native to South America. This species can grow up to 60 cm (24 inches) in disc width and weigh over 20 kg (44 lbs). Classified as "Endangered" by the IUCN Red List (2024), the species faces threats from habitat loss, pollution, and overfishing. Potamotrygon leopoldi is known for its distinctive black coloration with white spots, which serves as camouflage against the riverbed.

Life Style Summary

The Black Devil Stingray is a bottom-dwelling predator that primarily feeds on fish, crustaceans, and mollusks. It uses its electroreception to detect prey hidden under sand or silt. In the wild, these stingrays are solitary, territorial creatures. In captivity, they require a large aquarium with sandy substrate, strong water flow, and pristine water conditions to mimic their natural habitat. Their diet should consist of meaty foods like shrimp, fish, and earthworms.

Interesting Fact

Unlike their marine relatives, freshwater stingrays like the Black Devil have adapted to live in freshwater by evolving unique osmoregulatory mechanisms that enable them to balance the salt concentration in their bodies. These stingrays have specialized kidneys that help conserve essential salts and excrete excess water, allowing them to thrive in rivers far from the ocean.
