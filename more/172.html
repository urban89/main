Habitat Description

This species inhabits the rocky shores of Lake Malawi in East Africa. It thrives in clear, oxygen-rich waters, where it finds shelter among crevices and caves in the rocky substrate.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cichlidae, this species can reach up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length. Males are distinguished by their vibrant blue and black coloration, while females are more muted. The species is a maternal mouthbrooder, where females protect the eggs and fry in their mouths. As of 2024, the wild population remains stable with no immediate conservation concerns.

Life Style Summary

These fish are territorial, with males defending specific areas among rocks. They can be aggressive, especially toward other males, but can coexist with non-aggressive tank mates in sufficiently large environments. In the wild, they graze on algae and small invertebrates found on rocks. In captivity, they require a balanced diet of cichlid pellets, algae wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods.

Interesting Fact

The sharp, pointed teeth of this species give it the "dogtooth" nickname, enabling it to scrape algae from rocks efficiently, a vital adaptation for survival in its rocky environment.
