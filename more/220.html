Habitat Description

This species inhabits the shallow, slow-moving waters of the Amazon River basin, specifically in Peru, Brazil, and Bolivia. It prefers densely vegetated areas with a sandy or muddy substrate. The water is usually soft and acidic, with a temperature range of 24 to 28°C (75 to 82°F), providing a calm environment with plenty of hiding spots.

Scientific Facts

Part of the Cichlidae family, this dwarf cichlid is renowned for its vibrant colors and distinctive fin shapes, particularly the males' extended dorsal fins. Males typically reach up to 8 cm (3.1 inches) in length, while females are smaller. The species is not currently endangered, but local populations can be sensitive to habitat degradation (2024).

Life Style Summary

Known for their social and territorial behavior, they are best kept in pairs or harems in aquariums with plenty of hiding spots. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The species displays remarkable sexual dimorphism, with males showcasing bright red, orange, and blue colors and elaborately extended fins during courtship. This visual display is not only for attracting females but also for asserting dominance over other males.
