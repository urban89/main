Habitat Description

The Blue Danio is native to the freshwater streams and rivers of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand and Myanmar. These environments are characterized by clear, slow to moderately flowing water with sandy or rocky substrates and abundant aquatic vegetation. The water is usually soft to moderately hard and slightly acidic to neutral, with temperatures ranging from 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cyprinidae family, this species typically grows to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. It is recognized for its metallic blue body and striking horizontal stripes. As of 2024, it is not considered endangered, but habitat loss due to deforestation and pollution could threaten wild populations in the future. The Blue Danio is an egg scatterer, with eggs hatching in 24 to 36 hours without any parental care.

Life Style Summary

These fish are active and social, thriving in groups of six or more. They are fast swimmers and prefer to stay in the upper levels of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and organic material. In aquariums, they do well with a diet of high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and mosquito larvae.

Interesting Fact

Blue Danios are known for their unique spawning behavior, which involves rapid darting and circling near the water surface. This behavior helps disperse eggs over a wide area, increasing the chances of survival in the wild.
