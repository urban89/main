Habitat Description

The Myleus schomburgkii, commonly known as the Disk Tetra, is found in the rivers and floodplains of the Amazon and Orinoco basins in South America. It inhabits clear and blackwater environments with moderate to strong currents, where submerged vegetation and fallen branches provide cover. The water is typically soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging from 23-27°C (73-81°F).

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Serrasalmidae and is known for its disc-shaped body and silver coloration with distinctive black markings on its fins. Individuals can grow up to 25 cm (10 inches) in length. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN Red List, but local populations may be affected by habitat destruction and overfishing. Myleus schomburgkii is an omnivore, feeding on a diet of fruits, seeds, plant material, and small aquatic organisms.

Life Style Summary

The Disk Tetra is a social species that prefers to live in groups, exhibiting schooling behavior to avoid predators. It is an active swimmer, often found in the middle to upper levels of the water column. In captivity, it requires a large aquarium with plenty of swimming space, moderate water flow, and a diet that includes both plant matter and protein sources such as insects and small crustaceans.

Interesting Fact

The Disk Tetra has specialized teeth adapted for cutting through plant material, a trait that helps it consume a variety of foods in its natural habitat. This unique adaptation allows it to play a key role in seed dispersal for many riverbank plant species, contributing to the ecosystem's diversity.
