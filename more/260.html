Habitat Description

Trigonopoma pauciperforata, commonly known as the Red-Striped Rasbora, is native to slow-moving, blackwater rivers and peat swamps in Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaysia, Indonesia, and Thailand. These habitats are characterized by soft, acidic water rich in tannins, with temperatures ranging from 22°C to 26°C (72°F to 79°F). The dense vegetation and submerged plant material provide shelter and breeding grounds for this species.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, Red-Striped Rasboras typically reach about 5 cm (2 inches) in length. The species features a prominent red stripe running along the length of its body, from the snout to the tail. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status, although habitat loss due to deforestation and water pollution poses a growing threat.

Life Style Summary

Red-Striped Rasboras are peaceful, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. In the wild, they feed on small insects, zooplankton, and organic detritus. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia or brine shrimp. They are active swimmers, often seen moving throughout the middle layers of the water column.

Interesting Fact

The red stripe that gives this species its name is thought to play a role in mating displays, helping individuals stand out in their dark, tannin-rich natural environment. The coloration is most vivid under low lighting conditions, mimicking their native blackwater habitats.
