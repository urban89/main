Habitat Description

The Dwarf Pufferfish (Carinotetraodon travancoricus) is native to the freshwater rivers, lakes, and floodplains of Kerala and Karnataka in southwestern India. These fish inhabit slow-moving waters with dense aquatic vegetation, submerged roots, and leaf litter. The water is typically warm, slightly acidic to neutral, and rich in microfauna, which provides ample feeding opportunities.

Scientific Facts

Carinotetraodon travancoricus belongs to the family Tetraodontidae and is one of the smallest pufferfish species, reaching a maximum size of about 2.5 cm (1 inch). Unlike many pufferfish, the Dwarf Puffer is purely freshwater and does not require any brackish conditions. These fish are highly intelligent, displaying complex behaviors and interactions. They are listed as vulnerable in the wild due to habitat destruction and over-collection for the aquarium trade.

Life Style Summary

Dwarf Pufferfish are curious and territorial, often displaying unique behaviors such as hovering, investigating objects, and interacting with their environment. They prefer to live alone or in small groups, with a well-structured tank that includes plants, caves, and hiding spots. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, snails, and worms. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of live or frozen foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, and snails, which help to naturally trim their beaks.

Interesting Fact

Despite their small size, Dwarf Pufferfish have an impressive ability to recognize their owners and often display interactive behaviors, such as following fingers along the glass or eagerly swimming up during feeding time. Their distinctive eyes can move independently, allowing them to observe their surroundings with remarkable precision, making them both entertaining and captivating to watch.
