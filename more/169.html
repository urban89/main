Habitat Description

The Eureka Red Peacock (Aulonocara jacobfreibergi) is native to the rocky shores of Lake Malawi in East Africa. This species prefers clear, oxygen-rich waters with rocky crevices where it can seek shelter and forage. The lake's water is hard and alkaline, with minimal aquatic plants in its habitat.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cichlidae and can reach up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. Males are known for their brilliant red and blue colors, especially vibrant during mating displays. The species is a maternal mouthbrooder, where females incubate fertilized eggs in their mouths until they hatch. Populations in the wild are stable, with no significant threats to their conservation status (2024).

Life Style Summary

Eureka Red Peacocks are generally peaceful but can show territorial behavior, especially during breeding. They thrive in environments with rocky structures, where males establish territories. They are bottom dwellers, sifting through the sand to detect invertebrates using specialized sensory pores. In captivity, a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, occasional live foods, and vegetable matter is ideal.

Interesting Fact

The striking red and blue colors of the male Eureka Red Peacock serve a purpose in attracting females during breeding. The brighter the male, the higher the likelihood of attracting a mate, with colors intensifying when competing for females.
