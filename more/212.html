Habitat Description

Native to the freshwater rivers and streams of West Africa, primarily found in countries such as Senegal, Ghana, and Nigeria. It thrives in slow-moving waters with a sandy or muddy substrate, often surrounded by dense aquatic vegetation. The water in their natural habitat ranges in temperature from 24-28°C (75-82°F) and is slightly acidic to neutral, creating a suitable environment for this species.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cichlidae and is known for its vibrant coloration and territorial behavior. Hemichromis bimaculatus can grow up to 12-15 cm (4.7-6 inches) in length. It is easily recognizable by the two distinctive black spots located along its sides, which give the species its common name. As of now, the conservation status of the Jewel Cichlid is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024), indicating a stable population in the wild.

Life Style Summary

The Jewel Cichlid is a highly territorial species, especially during breeding season. They are best kept in pairs or species-specific tanks, as they can become aggressive towards other fish. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, crustaceans, and plant matter. In an aquarium setting, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms or brine shrimp. They are substrate spawners, laying their eggs on flat rocks or the aquarium glass.

Interesting Fact

Despite their vivid appearance, Jewel Cichlids possess a remarkable ability to change their colors depending on their mood, health, or environmental conditions. This color-shifting is a form of communication, often used to signal dominance, readiness to breed, or to deter potential threats. During breeding, their colors become even more intense.
