Habitat Description

Found in the deep, rocky regions of Lake Tanganyika, this species inhabits areas between 20 and 50 meters. It prefers sheltered spots among rocks and caves, where the water is clear, with temperatures ranging from 23-28°C (73-82°F) and a pH between 8.0 and 9.0.

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cichlidae family, Cyphotilapia frontosa is characterized by a distinct forehead hump that becomes more pronounced with age. They can reach up to 35 cm (14 inches) in length and are relatively slow-growing. Their conservation status is stable as of 2024, but threats like overfishing and habitat changes could affect their populations.

Life Style Summary

This species prefers to live in colonies, showing a relatively peaceful behavior compared to other large cichlids. In their natural environment, they hunt smaller fish and crustaceans. In aquariums, they need spacious tanks with plenty of rocky structures and caves. Their diet should include quality cichlid pellets, along with live or frozen foods such as shrimp or krill.

Interesting Fact

Known for their longevity, these fish can live up to 20 years or more in captivity with proper care. This long lifespan, along with their unique appearance and behavior, makes them a favorite among enthusiasts, who often selectively breed them for distinct color patterns and prominent nuchal humps.
