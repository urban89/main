Habitat Description

Trigonostigma hengeli, commonly known as the Glowlight Rasbora, is native to slow-moving streams and rivers in Southeast Asia, particularly in Borneo and Sumatra. These waters are often shaded by dense forest canopies, leading to soft, slightly acidic water with temperatures ranging from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F). The natural habitat is characterized by leaf litter, submerged roots, and dimly lit environments.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, Glowlight Rasboras typically grow to about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches) in length. Trigonostigma hengeli is a small, peaceful schooling fish that displays a striking orange-gold stripe running along its body. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" in the wild, though habitat degradation in some regions may pose a future threat.

Life Style Summary

Glowlight Rasboras are social fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. In the wild, they feed on small insects, zooplankton, and organic debris. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and live or frozen foods such as daphnia or brine shrimp. They are active swimmers that occupy the middle levels of the aquarium.

Interesting Fact

The bright, glowing stripe that characterizes Glowlight Rasboras serves as a form of camouflage in their natural environment. In the dimly lit waters of their habitat, this stripe mimics the appearance of sunlight filtering through the water, helping them blend in and avoid predators.
