Habitat Description

Etroplus suratensis is native to the brackish and freshwater rivers, lakes, and estuaries along the coasts of India and Sri Lanka. It thrives in environments with submerged vegetation and areas with slow-moving waters. Water temperatures range from 24°C to 30°C (75°F to 86°F), and the species can tolerate salinity fluctuations, which makes it highly adaptable to both fresh and brackish water habitats.

Scientific Facts

The Green Chromide, a member of the family Cichlidae, can grow up to 40 cm (16 inches) in length, making it one of the larger species in its genus. It is classified as "Vulnerable" due to habitat loss and overfishing for local consumption (2024). Despite its size, it exhibits peaceful behavior compared to other cichlids. This species is also known for its oval-shaped body covered in faint green stripes, which provide camouflage in its natural environment.

Life Style Summary

This species lives in schools and is highly social. In the wild, its diet consists of plant material, algae, and detritus. In aquariums, it needs a varied diet, including high-quality vegetable-based foods such as spirulina flakes, along with occasional protein-rich supplements. The Green Chromide spawns in shallow waters, with both parents providing care for the eggs and young fry.

Interesting Fact

This species has cultural significance in India, where it is locally referred to as "Pearlspot" and is a popular food fish. Its adaptability to both fresh and brackish waters has made it a key species in aquaculture programs aimed at improving local fisheries.
