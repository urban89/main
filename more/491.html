Habitat Description

The Yellow Phantom Tetra (Hyphessobrycon roseus) inhabits upper reaches of tributary drainages and igarapés (watercourse in the Amazon rainforest) in areas of tropical forests of Suriname and French Guiana. These waters are typically soft and slightly acidic, flowing through areas dense with aquatic vegetation. The natural environment provides plenty of shaded spots due to the overhanging vegetation, which helps diffuse the sunlight.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Characidae, the Yellow Phantom Tetra is closely related to other popular tetra species. It grows to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. The species is known for its distinct yellowish hue with a darker dorsal fin. It is currently not listed as endangered or vulnerable in its natural habitat. These fish are egg scatterers, and like many tetras, they do not exhibit parental care, with eggs typically hatching within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

Yellow Phantom Tetras are peaceful and prefer to live in schools of six or more. They are active and often seen swimming in the middle levels of the tank. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates, algae, and detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

The Yellow Phantom Tetra is relatively new to the aquarium trade and is less common than its Red Phantom counterpart. Its subtle coloration can shift depending on the lighting and mood, making it a fascinating species to observe in a well-planted aquarium.
