Habitat Description

This species is native to the slow-moving, tannin-rich waters of the Amazon Basin in South America, specifically in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. These environments are warm, soft, and acidic, with dense aquatic vegetation and submerged roots providing plenty of cover. The habitat is typically shaded by thick tree canopies, which reduce light penetration into the water.

Scientific Facts

Hypselecara temporalis belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 30 cm (12 inches) in length. Known for its striking ability to change coloration depending on its mood, the fish can range from a rich chocolate-brown to darker hues when stressed. First described in 1840, this species is not currently endangered (2024), although habitat loss and water pollution in its native range pose risks.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful for their size but may become territorial during breeding. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and organic detritus. In an aquarium, they thrive on a diet of high-quality pellets, flakes, and live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp. Their optimal temperature range is 24-28°C (75-82°F), and they need large tanks with stable water conditions.

Interesting Fact

One of the unique traits of this species is its ability to dramatically change color in response to its environment or mood, making it a fascinating fish to observe. This color shift is often most noticeable during breeding or when the fish feels threatened.
