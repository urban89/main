Habitat Description

The Red Eye Tetra (Moenkhausia sanctaefilomenae) is found in the freshwater rivers and streams of South America, primarily in the Paraguay, Paraná, and São Francisco river basins. These fish inhabit slow to moderate-flow waters with a sandy or muddy substrate, often surrounded by dense vegetation and submerged wood. The water in these areas is usually clear to slightly turbid and ranges from slightly acidic to neutral.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Characidae and is easily recognized by the distinctive red marking around its eyes, which contrasts with its silver body. Red Eye Tetras typically reach a length of about 7 cm (2.8 inches). They are egg scatterers, showing no parental care, and the eggs hatch within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, the species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in its natural habitat with no significant threats.

Life Style Summary

Red Eye Tetras are social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers, often found in the middle levels of the aquarium. In their natural habitat, they feed on insects, small crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they are best fed with a variety of flake foods, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

The bright red ring around the eye of this species serves as a form of social signaling among their group, helping them maintain school cohesion. This adaptation is particularly useful in murky waters, where visual signals are essential for group navigation and predator avoidance.
