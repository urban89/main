Habitat Description

The Pink-tail Chalceus (Chalceus macrolepidotus) is native to the fast-flowing rivers and streams of the Amazon Basin, primarily in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. It thrives in clear waters with moderate to strong currents, often found in areas with submerged roots, rocks, and driftwood, which provide shelter and hunting grounds.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Characidae, recognized for its elongated body and vibrant pink caudal fin. Pink-tail Chalceus can reach a length of up to 20 cm (8 inches). They are primarily carnivorous, feeding on insects, small fish, and crustaceans. In nature, their populations are considered stable, with no current evidence of significant endangerment (2024).

Life Style Summary

The Pink-tail Chalceus is a moderately aggressive fish that requires ample swimming space and a well-structured environment to establish territories. It prefers to occupy the upper to middle layers of the water column and is known for its active and agile swimming behavior. In captivity, a varied diet consisting of high-quality pellets, live, or frozen foods like bloodworms and small shrimp is recommended to maintain health and coloration.

Interesting Fact

This species has an unusual adaptation among characins: it possesses a bony tongue covered with teeth, allowing it to grasp and manipulate prey with precision. This feature is believed to have evolved to help the fish hunt effectively in fast-moving waters, where its speed and agility are crucial for catching prey.
