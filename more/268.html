Habitat Description

Danio albolineatus, commonly known as the Pearl Danio, originates from slow-moving streams and rivers in Southeast Asia, particularly in Myanmar, Thailand, and Sumatra. These waters are often clear and shaded by dense vegetation, providing soft, slightly acidic to neutral water with a temperature range of 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, Pearl Danios typically grow up to 5 cm (2 inches) in length. The species is known for its shimmering pearlescent coloration, which is most visible in natural light. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status, though habitat loss in some regions poses a long-term threat.

Life Style Summary

Pearl Danios are peaceful schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, larvae, and zooplankton. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like daphnia and bloodworms. They are active swimmers that occupy the middle to upper levels of the aquarium.

Interesting Fact

The pearlescent sheen of the Pearl Danio is not only attractive but also serves as a form of camouflage in their natural environment. This shimmering effect helps them blend into their surroundings by reflecting light in a way that confuses predators, especially in dappled sunlight through water.
