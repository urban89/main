Habitat Description

Found in the open waters of Lake Tanganyika, this species inhabits the midwater zones, typically at depths of 10 to 40 meters. It prefers areas with clear, deep water, where it forms large schools. The temperature in these regions ranges from 23-27°C (73-81°F) with a pH between 8.0 and 9.0, reflecting the stable, mineral-rich conditions of the lake.

Scientific Facts

Cyprichromis leptosoma belongs to the family Cichlidae and is known for its streamlined body, allowing it to navigate the open water efficiently. Males can reach up to 11 cm (4.3 inches) in length, while females are slightly smaller. This species is unique among cichlids due to its pelagic lifestyle and its preference for living in the middle to upper water columns. As of 2024, it is not considered endangered, but localized populations could be impacted by overfishing and environmental changes.

Life Style Summary

These fish are social and form large, tight-knit schools, often displaying striking color variations among males. In their natural habitat, they feed on plankton and small invertebrates. In aquariums, they require a spacious setup with open swimming areas and a diet of high-quality cichlid flakes, micro-pellets, and small live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or daphnia. They are peaceful and do well with other non-aggressive, open-water species.

Interesting Fact

The species exhibits a fascinating reproductive behavior known as “maternal mouthbrooding.” After spawning, the female collects the fertilized eggs in her mouth, where she incubates them for several weeks until the fry are ready to swim freely. This adaptation provides protection for the developing young in the open water.
