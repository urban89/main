Habitat Description

The Cardinal Tetra (Paracheirodon axelrodi) inhabits the blackwater and clearwater streams of the Amazon Basin, particularly in the Rio Negro and Orinoco River systems. These fish prefer soft, acidic waters with temperatures ranging from 23-27°C (73-81°F). Their environment is shaded by dense rainforest canopies, with a substrate rich in decaying leaves and organic debris.

Scientific Facts

As a member of the family Characidae, the species grows up to 5 cm (2 inches) and is known for its vibrant blue and red stripes that run along the entire body. It is more sensitive to water quality changes than other tetras and requires stable conditions. Classified as "Least Concern" in 2024, the fish faces potential threats from habitat loss and over-collection in the aquarium trade.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful and thrive in groups of six or more, swimming actively in the middle levels of the aquarium. In the wild, they consume small insects, larvae, and plant material. In captivity, they do well with a varied diet, including flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and bloodworms. They require soft, acidic water to mimic their natural habitat.

Interesting Fact

The striking coloration of Cardinal Tetras is believed to play a role in social communication within the school. The intensity of the blue and red stripes can change depending on the fish's mood and health, becoming more vivid when they are in a relaxed state and duller when stressed or sick.
