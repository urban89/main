Habitat Description

The Eartheater Cichlid (Geophagus altifrons) is found in the slow-moving rivers and floodplains of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil. These areas are characterized by soft, sandy substrates where the water is typically soft and slightly acidic. The natural habitat includes submerged plants, leaf litter, and ample cover, creating a diverse ecosystem.

Scientific Facts

Geophagus altifrons belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 25 cm (10 inches) in length. This species is known for its unique feeding behavior, where it sifts through the sand to find food particles, hence the name "Eartheater." It was first described in 1849 and is not currently considered endangered (2024), but habitat degradation remains a potential threat.

Life Style Summary

Eartheater Cichlids are peaceful but can be territorial during breeding. They thrive in aquariums with fine sand substrates and plenty of hiding places. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and organic detritus they sift from the sand. In captivity, they should be fed a diet that includes sinking pellets, frozen foods, and occasional live foods like bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

This species exhibits a unique form of parental care. After hatching, the fry are taken into the parents' mouths for protection, a behavior that helps ensure the survival of their young in environments with many predators.
