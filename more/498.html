Habitat Description

The White-Spotted Cichlid (Tropheus duboisi) is native to the rocky shorelines of Lake Tanganyika in East Africa. This lake is known for its crystal-clear, alkaline waters and rocky substrates. The species thrives in shallow waters where they graze on algae growing on rocks, and the environment is characterized by high levels of dissolved oxygen and stable water conditions.

Scientific Facts

Tropheus duboisi belongs to the Cichlidae family, known for its diverse and colorful species. They reach a maximum size of about 12 cm (4.7 inches). Juveniles are characterized by black bodies with white spots, while adults develop a blue-black coloration with a yellow band near their heads. This species is not currently endangered but faces habitat threats from pollution and overfishing in Lake Tanganyika. They are mouthbrooders, with females incubating eggs in their mouths until the fry are ready to swim freely.

Life Style Summary

White-Spotted Cichlids are shoaling fish that do best in groups of at least six individuals. They are territorial and can display aggression, especially during feeding and breeding. They spend much of their time grazing on algae and small organisms found on rocks. In captivity, they require a spacious tank with rocky decor and strong water flow to simulate their natural environment. A diet rich in vegetable matter, including spirulina and other algae-based foods, is essential to their health.

Interesting Fact

The White-Spotted Cichlid undergoes a dramatic color change as it matures, losing its juvenile spots and developing the adult's characteristic blue-black coloration with a yellow band. This color transformation makes it a favorite among aquarists, as the fish's appearance evolves significantly as it grows.
