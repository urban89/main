Habitat Description

The Dwarf Rasbora (Boraras maculatus) originates from the slow-moving blackwater streams, swamps, and peat bogs of Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaysia and Sumatra. These habitats are characterized by soft, acidic water tinted dark by tannins from decomposing plant material. The environment is typically shaded by dense vegetation, providing a dimly lit and highly organic-rich habitat.

Scientific Facts

Boraras maculatus belongs to the family Cyprinidae, which includes many small, colorful species commonly found in the aquarium trade. Dwarf Rasboras grow to a maximum size of about 2 cm (0.8 inches). They are easily identified by their small, slender bodies with reddish coloration and distinctive dark spots along their flanks. The species is currently not considered endangered, although habitat loss from deforestation and water pollution threatens their natural populations.

Life Style Summary

Dwarf Rasboras are peaceful, schooling fish that thrive in groups of at least six or more. They are active swimmers, often seen darting among plants and open spaces in the mid to upper levels of the aquarium. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, zooplankton, and other micro-organisms. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality micro-pellets, flake food, and occasional live or frozen foods like daphnia and microworms.

Interesting Fact

Despite their tiny size, Dwarf Rasboras are known for their vibrant color and active nature, making them a favorite among aquarists looking to add a pop of color to small aquariums or nano tanks. Their small size and peaceful behavior make them ideal tank mates for other similarly sized, non-aggressive species.
