Habitat Description

The Cyrtocara moorii, commonly known as the Blue Dolphin Cichlid, is native to the shallow, sandy-bottomed regions of Lake Malawi in East Africa. This species prefers open areas with plenty of swimming space and occasional rock formations. Water temperatures in its natural habitat range from 24-28°C (75-82°F), with a slightly alkaline pH level between 7.5 and 8.5.

Scientific Facts

This cichlid belongs to the family Cichlidae and is known for its distinctive hump on the forehead, which becomes more pronounced as the fish matures. It can reach a length of up to 20 cm (8 inches). As of 2024, Cyrtocara moorii is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN Red List, thanks to its widespread presence in Lake Malawi. The species is a mouthbrooder, with females carrying and protecting the eggs and fry in their mouths until they are ready to swim freely.

Life Style Summary

Blue Dolphin Cichlids are relatively peaceful for cichlids but can display territorial behavior during breeding. They are omnivorous, feeding on small invertebrates and organic matter stirred up from the sandy substrate by other fish. In captivity, they thrive in large aquariums with open swimming spaces, sandy substrates, and a varied diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp, and plant-based items like spirulina.

Interesting Fact

The Blue Dolphin Cichlid is known for its cooperative behavior with other fish species in its native habitat. It often follows behind larger fish like sand-sifting cichlids, feeding on small invertebrates and detritus that these fish disturb from the substrate, demonstrating a unique form of mutualistic interaction.
