Habitat Description

The Knodus borki is native to the clear, slow-moving streams and rivers of the upper Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in Brazil and Peru. These habitats are typically shaded by dense forest canopy, which limits light penetration and creates a dim environment. The water is soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging from 22-28°C (72-82°F), providing an ideal setting for this small schooling fish.

Scientific Facts

Blue Tetras belong to the family Characidae and typically grow to about 5 cm (2 inches) in length. The species is recognized for its unique metallic blue sheen that covers the body, making it an attractive addition to community aquariums. As of 2024, the Blue Tetra is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating stable populations in its native habitats. They are egg scatterers with no parental care, and their eggs usually hatch within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These tetras are active and social, thriving in groups of six or more. They prefer to occupy the middle to upper levels of the water column and are compatible with other peaceful fish. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The Blue Tetra has a remarkable ability to change the intensity of its color depending on its mood and environment. When feeling threatened or stressed, it may lose some of its vibrant blue color, becoming more subdued. This color change serves as a unique form of communication among its species and as a signal for aquarists to check on their aquarium conditions.
