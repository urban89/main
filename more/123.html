Habitat Description

The Hyphessobrycon pulchripinnis, commonly known as the Lemon Tetra, is native to the clear, slow-moving waters of the Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in the tributaries of Brazil. These environments are characterized by dense vegetation, submerged roots, and fallen leaves, creating a dimly lit habitat with soft, slightly acidic water. The water temperature typically ranges from 22-28°C (72-82°F).

Scientific Facts

Lemon Tetras belong to the family Characidae and typically grow to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. They are recognized by their translucent yellow body and bright red eyes. As of 2024, this species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating stable populations in the wild. Lemon Tetras are egg scatterers with no parental care, and their eggs generally hatch within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These tetras are peaceful and prefer to live in schools of six or more. They are active swimmers, occupying the middle to upper levels of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant material. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

The vibrant yellow coloration of the Lemon Tetra is not just for show; it plays a role in social signaling and mate selection. Males with brighter colors are often more attractive to females, making their vivid hue a key factor in their reproductive success.
