Habitat Description

The Stiphodon semoni, commonly known as the Cobalt Blue Goby, is found in the fast-flowing, clear streams and rivers of Southeast Asia and the western Pacific, particularly in Indonesia, Papua New Guinea, and the Solomon Islands. These habitats are characterized by rocky substrates, high oxygen levels, and a constant flow of water. The water temperature ranges from 22-28°C (72-82°F), and the environment is typically well-oxygenated with a slightly alkaline pH.

Scientific Facts

Cobalt Blue Gobies are members of the family Gobiidae and typically reach a length of 5 to 7 cm (2-2.8 inches). They are known for their striking cobalt blue coloration, especially in males. As of 2024, this species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to their stable populations across a wide distribution. They are egg layers that deposit eggs on rocks or other hard surfaces, where the male guards them until they hatch in about 5 to 7 days.

Life Style Summary

Cobalt Blue Gobies are peaceful, social fish that do well in groups, but they can be territorial with their own kind, particularly males. They are primarily bottom dwellers, spending most of their time grazing on biofilm, algae, and small invertebrates. In captivity, they require a diet of algae wafers, high-quality sinking pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia and bloodworms. These fish thrive in tanks with strong water flow and plenty of rocks or wood for grazing.

Interesting Fact

The Cobalt Blue Goby has a unique method of climbing waterfalls and navigating swift currents in its natural habitat. Using its pelvic fins, which form a sucker-like disc, the fish can cling to rocks and surfaces, allowing it to move against the flow of water with ease, a remarkable adaptation that aids its survival in fast-flowing streams.
