Habitat Description

The Altum Angelfish (Pterophyllum altum) is native to the slow-moving, tannin-rich waters of the Orinoco and Amazon River basins in South America, particularly in Venezuela and Colombia. These waters are soft, acidic, and warm, with submerged roots and dense vegetation providing the ideal environment for this species to thrive.

Scientific Facts

This species, belonging to the family Cichlidae, is known for its distinctive tall, slender body and long fins. Altum Angelfish can grow up to 20 cm (8 inches) in height and 15 cm (6 inches) in length. First described in 1903, it is not currently considered endangered (2024), though habitat degradation from deforestation and water pollution poses a risk to wild populations.

Life Style Summary

Altum Angelfish are generally peaceful, although they can become territorial during breeding. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and organic detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality pellets, flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp. They prefer to live in groups and require tall, spacious aquariums to accommodate their height.

Interesting Fact

Due to its taller, more streamlined body compared to other species in its genus, the Altum Angelfish is exceptionally well-adapted for gliding through densely packed roots and submerged vegetation, helping it evade predators in its native habitat.
