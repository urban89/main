Habitat Description

The Pale Usisya Aulonocara (Aulonocara steveni), also known as Aulonocara Blue Neon, is found in the rocky areas of Lake Malawi in East Africa. It inhabits the shallow waters near sandy and rocky substrates, where it uses crevices in the rocks to hide from predators and forage for food in the sand.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cichlidae and is recognized for its brilliant blue coloration, especially in males. It can grow up to 12 cm (5 inches) in length. As a member of the "peacock cichlids," it is known for its specialized sensory pores on the head, which help detect prey in the sand. The population of this species in the wild remains stable, with no current conservation concerns (2024).

Life Style Summary

Pale Usisya Aulonocara are relatively peaceful and territorial only during breeding. They prefer to spend their time near the bottom of the water column, using their sensory capabilities to sift through sand in search of small invertebrates. In captivity, they do well with a mixed diet of pellets, flake food, and occasional live or frozen treats like brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

This species is known for its unique ability to "listen" to vibrations in the sand using the sensory pores on its head. This adaptation allows it to detect movement and locate prey that is hidden beneath the substrate, making it a highly efficient hunter even in murky environments.
