Habitat Description

The Poecilia wingei is native to the freshwater lagoons and small streams of northeastern Venezuela, particularly around the Paria Peninsula. These waters are warm, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F), and are usually clear with a slightly alkaline pH. The habitat is characterized by dense vegetation, both submerged and floating, providing ample hiding places and a rich environment for small invertebrates, their primary food source.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Poeciliidae and is closely related to the common guppy. Males are small, typically growing to about 2.5 cm (1 inch), while females are slightly larger, reaching up to 4 cm (1.6 inches). They are livebearers, giving birth to fully formed young instead of laying eggs. The species is currently listed as "Vulnerable" by the IUCN (2024) due to habitat destruction and pollution in their native range.

Life Style Summary

Endler's Livebearers are active, social fish that thrive in groups, with a balanced ratio of males to females to reduce stress. They inhabit the upper and middle levels of the water column and are constantly on the move. In the wild, they feed on small insects, algae, and plant material. In aquariums, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Endler's Livebearers were rediscovered in 1975 by John Endler, after whom they were named. Unlike many other fish, males are known for their rapid and vivid color changes, a trait believed to have evolved to attract females and evade predators by blending into different backgrounds.
