Habitat Description

The Marbled Hatchetfish (Carnegiella strigata) is native to the slow-moving, shaded streams and tributaries of the Amazon Basin in South America, primarily in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. They are typically found in soft, acidic water with dense vegetation and floating plants. The natural habitat is characterized by dark, tannin-stained waters with a sandy or muddy substrate, providing plenty of cover and feeding opportunities.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Gasteropelecidae and is recognized for its unique hatchet-shaped body, which allows it to leap out of the water to escape predators. Marbled Hatchetfish reach a size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They are surface dwellers and egg scatterers, providing no parental care, with eggs hatching within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in the wild.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful and do best in groups of six or more, staying near the surface where they can quickly dart away from threats. They primarily feed on small insects and larvae that fall onto the water's surface. In captivity, they require a diet of high-quality flake foods, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like mosquito larvae and fruit flies.

Interesting Fact

The Marbled Hatchetfish has a unique ability to jump out of the water by flapping its pectoral fins, similar to how a bird flaps its wings. This rare adaptation allows it to escape predators and catch flying insects just above the water's surface, demonstrating a remarkable survival strategy in its native habitat.
