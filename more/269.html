Habitat Description

The Celestial Pearl Danio (Danio margaritatus) is native to small, shallow ponds in the Shan State of Myanmar. These ponds are typically clear and heavily vegetated, with a soft, sandy substrate. The water in these habitats is slightly alkaline and moderate in temperature, usually ranging between 22-26°C (72-79°F). The natural environment provides ample cover with aquatic plants and floating vegetation, which helps protect these small fish from predators.

Scientific Facts

Celestial Pearl Danios belong to the family Cyprinidae and are small, vibrantly colored fish, usually reaching about 2-2.5 cm (0.8-1 inch) in length. They were first discovered in 2006 and quickly became popular in the aquarium trade due to their striking appearance. The species is characterized by a deep blue body adorned with pearl-like spots and bright red fins. Danio margaritatus is not currently endangered, but its limited natural range and the popularity in the aquarium trade have raised concerns about overcollection.

Life Style Summary

Celestial Pearl Danios are peaceful and shy fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers but prefer to stay close to vegetation where they feel secure. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and other microscopic organisms. In captivity, they should be offered a varied diet of high-quality micro-pellets, crushed flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia or brine shrimp. Although small, these fish exhibit fascinating social behaviors. Males often establish small territories within the vegetation and display their bright colors to attract females or to ward off rival males. They engage in gentle, ritualized combat that rarely causes harm. When kept in well-planted aquariums, Celestial Pearl Danios exhibit more natural behaviors and are more likely to breed, with males constantly patrolling their territories and performing elaborate courtship dances.
