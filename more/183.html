Habitat Description

This cichlid originates from Lake Malawi, particularly around the rocky areas of Likoma Island. It thrives in shallow, rocky habitats with warm, clear, and slightly alkaline water. The temperature in its natural environment ranges between 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F), providing ideal conditions for its survival.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cichlidae, this species grows up to 10 cm (4 inches) for males, with females being slightly smaller. As of (2024), it is classified as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status. The fish is a maternal mouthbrooder, where females carry and protect the eggs and fry in their mouths until they can swim on their own.

Life Style Summary

This cichlid is highly territorial and aggressive, particularly during the breeding season. It prefers rocky environments where it can establish territories. In the wild, its diet consists of small invertebrates and algae. In captivity, it requires a protein-rich diet including pellets, frozen foods, and live prey. An aquarium with plenty of rocky formations is necessary to replicate its natural behavior.

Interesting Fact

This species was named after Joan Johnson, a key figure in cichlid research. Males display vibrant blue and black hues during breeding season, signaling their dominance and readiness to mate.
