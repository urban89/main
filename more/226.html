Habitat Description

The Bolivian Ram (Mikrogeophagus altispinosa) is native to the slow-moving waters of the Amazon River basin, particularly in Bolivia and Brazil. It inhabits shallow rivers, streams, and floodplains with soft, sandy substrates. These areas are often rich in vegetation and organic debris, creating slightly acidic and soft water conditions.

Scientific Facts

Mikrogeophagus altispinosa belongs to the family Cichlidae and typically grows to around 7 cm (2.8 inches) in length. This species was first described in 1911 and is known for its peaceful nature compared to other cichlids. The Bolivian Ram is not considered endangered (2024), but like many species in the Amazon, habitat loss due to deforestation could pose a future threat.

Life Style Summary

Bolivian Rams are social fish and prefer to live in pairs or small groups. They are bottom dwellers and enjoy sifting through the substrate for food. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality pellets, frozen foods, and occasional live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp. These cichlids are known for their relatively peaceful temperament, making them suitable for community tanks.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many cichlids, Bolivian Rams exhibit biparental care. Both male and female fish actively guard and care for their offspring, showing a remarkable level of coordination during the breeding process.
