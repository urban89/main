Habitat Description

This species originates from the slow-moving rivers and floodplains of the Amazon Basin, primarily in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. These waters are soft and slightly acidic, with dense aquatic vegetation and submerged roots providing ample cover. Overhanging canopies filter sunlight, creating a dimly lit environment in which the fish thrive.

Scientific Facts

Pterophyllum scalare belongs to the family Cichlidae and can reach a body length of up to 15 cm (6 inches), with fin extensions reaching 20 cm (8 inches). First described in 1823, this species is known for its unique, tall body shape and flowing fins. Although not currently endangered (2024), habitat destruction and pollution in its native region are potential threats.

Life Style Summary

These fish are generally peaceful but can become territorial during breeding. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and detritus. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flakes, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp. They form strong monogamous pairs and are known for their parental care, guarding both eggs and fry.

Interesting Fact

With their tall, thin bodies, these fish are perfectly adapted to navigating through dense aquatic vegetation, allowing them to evade predators and forage in narrow spaces that other species cannot reach.
