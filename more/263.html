Habitat Description

Rasbora einthovenii, commonly known as the Brilliant Rasbora, is native to slow-moving streams, rivers, and flooded forests in Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo. These habitats are typically rich in vegetation, with soft, slightly acidic water. The water temperature ranges from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F), with dense plant cover providing both shelter and breeding grounds.

Scientific Facts

Brilliant Rasboras belong to the family Cyprinidae and typically grow to about 6 cm (2.4 inches) in length. This species is recognized for its vibrant, metallic sheen that reflects under natural light, earning it the name "Brilliant" Rasbora. Currently, the species is listed as "Least Concern" in the wild (2024), though deforestation and habitat degradation could pose a future threat.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful, active swimmers that thrive in schools of six or more. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, zooplankton, and organic detritus. In captivity, they do well with high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp. They tend to occupy the middle and upper levels of the aquarium, making them ideal for well-planted tanks.

Interesting Fact

The reflective scales of the Brilliant Rasbora help it blend into its natural environment by mimicking the dappled sunlight filtering through the water's surface. This not only aids in camouflage but is also believed to play a role in attracting mates during breeding season.
