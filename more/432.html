Habitat Description

The Etheostoma zonale is typically found in the clear, fast-flowing streams and small rivers of the eastern United States, particularly in the Ohio River basin. These habitats are characterized by rocky or gravel substrates and abundant submerged vegetation, which provide shelter and feeding grounds. The water is generally cool, ranging from 10-20°C (50-68°F), and well-oxygenated.

Scientific Facts

The Banded Darter belongs to the family Percidae and grows up to 7 cm (2.8 inches) in length. It is distinguished by its bright green and blue bands that run vertically along its body. As of 2024, this species is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to its stable populations across its native range. The fish are egg layers, depositing their eggs on gravel or sand substrates, where they hatch in about 5 to 10 days, depending on water temperature.

Life Style Summary

Banded Darters are bottom-dwelling fish that prefer to live in groups, though they are not strictly schooling. They are active during the day, feeding primarily on small invertebrates such as insect larvae, crustaceans, and worms. In captivity, they do well in well-oxygenated aquariums with strong water flow and should be fed a varied diet of live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

The species uses a unique method of locomotion, relying on short, rapid bursts of swimming to dart between rocks and crevices, which helps them evade predators.
