Habitat Description

Rhodeus amarus is native to slow-moving or still freshwater habitats in Europe and parts of Asia, including rivers, lakes, and ponds. These waters typically have dense vegetation and soft, muddy substrates. The temperature in their natural environment ranges from 10°C to 25°C (50°F to 77°F), with slightly alkaline water conditions.

Scientific Facts

European Bitterlings belong to the family Cyprinidae and usually reach a length of 5–7 cm (2–3 inches). What makes this species unique is its breeding behavior: the female deposits her eggs inside live freshwater mussels, where the young develop in a protected environment. As of 2024, the species is listed as "Least Concern," but local populations may be impacted by water pollution and habitat degradation.

Life Style Summary

Bitterlings are social fish that prefer to live in groups. In their natural habitat, they feed on algae, small invertebrates, and organic detritus. In aquariums, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. They are peaceful and prefer well-planted tanks with stable water conditions.

Interesting Fact

The unique reproductive strategy of the Bitterling, where it uses freshwater mussels for egg protection, has evolved as a survival tactic. This behavior provides a secure environment for the eggs, protecting them from predators and other external threats.
