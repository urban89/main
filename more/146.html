Habitat Description

The Hockeystick Pencilfish (Nannostomus eques) is native to the slow-moving streams and tributaries of the Amazon River basin in South America, particularly in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. This species is typically found in areas with dense vegetation and submerged roots. The natural habitat has a soft, sandy or muddy substrate, slightly acidic water, and is stained with tannins from decaying organic matter, creating a tea-colored environment with low light penetration.

Scientific Facts

Hockeystick Pencilfish belong to the family Lebiasinidae and are known for their elongated bodies and distinctive diagonal swimming posture, which resembles the shape of a hockey stick. They grow to an average size of 5 cm (2 inches). Nannostomus eques is a peaceful, schooling fish that prefers to swim at an angle in the upper levels of the water column. This species is not currently considered endangered, but its natural habitats are threatened by deforestation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Hockeystick Pencilfish are social creatures that thrive in groups of six or more. They are most active during daylight hours, swimming near the water's surface at an upward angle, which helps them access food like small insects and plankton. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. A well-planted tank with gentle water flow and subdued lighting is ideal for this species.

Interesting Fact

The Hockeystick Pencilfish is known for its unusual swimming style, often maintaining a head-up position at a 45-degree angle. This behavior helps them remain inconspicuous to predators and catch floating prey more effectively. Their unique posture and vibrant red and black stripes make them a fascinating addition to any aquarium.
