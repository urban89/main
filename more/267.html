Habitat Description

The Red-tailed Rasbora (Rasbora borapetensis) is native to Southeast Asia, primarily found in Thailand, Malaysia, and Cambodia. It inhabits clear, slow-moving streams, ponds, and rivers with dense vegetation and sandy or muddy substrates. These waters are typically neutral to slightly acidic, with moderate levels of light penetration due to surrounding foliage.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, this species is recognized for its slender body, which can grow up to 5 cm (2 inches) in length. Red-tailed Rasboras are schooling fish that exhibit bright silver bodies with a distinctive red or orange tail. They are egg scatterers and show no parental care for their offspring. According to the IUCN Red List, this species is listed as Least Concern (2024), indicating stable populations in their native habitats.

Life Style Summary

Red-tailed Rasboras are active, peaceful fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are mid to upper-level swimmers in an aquarium and prefer an environment that mimics their natural habitat, including ample swimming space and live plants. In the wild, they feed on small insects, worms, and plant matter. In captivity, a diet of high-quality flake foods, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen food helps maintain their health and vibrant colors.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many other rasbora species, the Red-tailed Rasbora has adapted to living in both blackwater and clearer water environments, making them versatile in aquariums. Their striking coloration is often enhanced by their natural diet, which includes carotenoid-rich foods that help maintain the vivid red hue of their tails.
