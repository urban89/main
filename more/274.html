Habitat Description

The Spotted Danio (Danio nigrofasciatus) is native to the freshwater streams and rivers of Myanmar. These environments typically have clear, shallow water with moderate flow and are rich in aquatic vegetation and submerged rocks. The natural habitat is characterized by soft, slightly acidic to neutral water with temperatures ranging from 20°C to 26°C (68°F to 79°F).

Scientific Facts

Spotted Danios are part of the Cyprinidae family, which includes many small, hardy species. They usually grow to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. The species is recognized for its distinctive spotted pattern along its body, interspersed with horizontal stripes. These fish are egg scatterers that show no parental care, with their eggs hatching in approximately 48 hours. As of 2024, the species is not considered endangered, but habitat degradation in their native range poses potential risks to wild populations.

Life Style Summary

Spotted Danios are active and schooling fish, best kept in groups of at least six to ensure they feel secure and display their natural behaviors. They occupy the middle to upper levels of the aquarium, often darting around in search of food or exploring their environment. In the wild, they feed on small insects, worms, and plankton. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

They can survive in environments with fluctuating water levels. During the dry season, they can be found in isolated pools or small, slow-moving streams, where they are known to jump between shallow water bodies to find food or escape predators. This ability to leap short distances out of water is not just a survival mechanism but also contributes to their natural behavior of exploring their surroundings.
