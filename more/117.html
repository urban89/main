Habitat Description

The Glass Bloodfin Tetra (Prionobrama filigera) is found in the clear, slow-moving freshwater rivers and streams of South America, specifically in the Paraguay, Paraná, and Guaporé river basins. These waters are typically clear or slightly tinted with tannins, and they have a moderate to soft flow with plenty of aquatic vegetation. The species thrives in habitats with a moderate current, ample plant cover, and a substrate composed of fine sand or mud.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Characidae and is known for its transparent body, through which its internal organs are visible. Glass Bloodfin Tetras usually grow to a length of 4-5 cm (1.5-2 inches). They are peaceful, schooling fish and prefer to swim in the mid to upper levels of the water column. As of 2024, they are classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in their natural habitat without any immediate threat to their conservation status.

Life Style Summary

They are highly social and thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and spend most of their time in the middle to upper layers of the aquarium. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, crustaceans, and other zooplankton. In captivity, they should be fed a varied diet, including flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia and mosquito larvae.

Interesting Fact

The Glass Bloodfin Tetra is known for its remarkable schooling behavior. When threatened, these fish exhibit a unique survival tactic: they use their semi-transparent bodies to blend in with their surroundings, making it difficult for predators to focus on individual fish. This camouflage technique is particularly effective in dense vegetation or when swimming in large groups.
