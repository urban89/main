Habitat Description

The Threadfin Acara (Acarichthys heckelii) is native to the slow-moving waters of the Amazon and Orinoco River basins in South America. These fish thrive in areas with sandy substrates, submerged roots, and leaf litter, where the water is soft and slightly acidic. They are often found in shallow, quiet pools and streams.

Scientific Facts

Acarichthys heckelii belongs to the family Cichlidae and can reach a length of up to 15 cm (6 inches). It was first described in 1840 and is known for the long, filamentous extensions on its dorsal and caudal fins, giving it the name "Threadfin Acara." The species is not considered endangered (2024), although its habitat is subject to environmental pressures from deforestation and pollution.

Life Style Summary

Threadfin Acaras are moderately territorial but generally peaceful, especially when not breeding. They prefer to dwell in the middle to lower levels of the aquarium, exploring the substrate and digging for food. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates and plant material. In captivity, they can be fed high-quality pellets, frozen foods, and live foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many cichlids, Acarichthys heckelii digs complex tunnel systems in sandy substrates where it shelters and spawns. This behavior helps protect its eggs and fry from predators.
