Habitat Description

The Dwarf Mbuna (Pseudotropheus demasoni) is native to the rocky shores of Lake Malawi, specifically around Pombo Rocks. It thrives in shallow waters, typically found at depths of 2 to 5 meters, where it inhabits rocky crevices and caves. The water is clear and alkaline, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a pH between 7.8 and 8.6.

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cichlidae family, this small but aggressive cichlid can grow up to 8 cm (3 inches) in length. Known for its vibrant blue and black vertical stripes, Pseudotropheus demasoni is highly territorial despite its small size. As of 2024, it is not endangered in the wild, but localized populations could be affected by habitat changes and collection for the aquarium trade.

Life Style Summary

This mbuna cichlid is highly territorial and best kept in species-specific tanks or with other robust mbuna species. It thrives in aquariums with rocky setups that mimic its natural habitat, providing numerous hiding places. Its diet consists mainly of algae, but it also accepts high-quality cichlid pellets, spirulina, and vegetable-based foods. Due to its aggressive nature, it requires careful management of tank mates and territory.

Interesting Fact

Despite its small size, the Dwarf Mbuna exhibits intense territorial behavior. Males defend their territory vigorously, often claiming a small area of rocks as their own. This behavior helps them establish dominance and attract females, but it also means they need plenty of space and visual barriers in the aquarium to minimize aggression.
