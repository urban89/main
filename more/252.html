Habitat Description

Epalzeorhynchos kalopterus, commonly known as the Flying Fox, is native to the fast-flowing streams and rivers of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. These habitats are rich in vegetation and rocky substrates, providing plenty of algae and biofilm for the species to feed on. Water temperatures typically range from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F), with slightly acidic to neutral water conditions.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. The Flying Fox is not currently at risk of extinction and is listed as "Least Concern" in the wild (2024). It is known for its distinctive black stripe that runs from its snout to the tail, bordered by golden highlights, giving it a sleek, streamlined appearance suited for fast-flowing waters.

Life Style Summary

Flying Foxes are semi-aggressive and territorial, particularly with their own kind, though they can coexist with similarly sized fish. In the wild, their diet consists mainly of algae, periphyton, and small invertebrates. In captivity, they require a diet that includes algae wafers, vegetable matter, and protein-rich foods such as brine shrimp or bloodworms. A well-planted aquarium with plenty of hiding spots is ideal for them.

Interesting Fact

The Flying Fox is often mistaken for the Siamese Algae Eater, but unlike the latter, this species does not consume black beard algae, making it less effective at controlling certain types of algae in aquariums.
