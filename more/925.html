Habitat Description

The Lymnaea stagnalis is commonly found in freshwater habitats across Europe, North America, and parts of Asia. It thrives in still or slow-moving waters such as ponds, lakes, marshes, and canals. These environments are usually rich in vegetation, which provides both food and shelter. The water is often neutral to slightly alkaline, with temperatures ranging from 15-25°C (59-77°F).

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Lymnaeidae, this species is one of the largest freshwater snails, reaching up to 7 cm (2.8 inches) in shell length. It has a thin, translucent shell that is coiled and elongated, with a large aperture. As of 2024, the Great Pond Snail is considered "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to its wide distribution and adaptability to different freshwater habitats. It is a hermaphroditic species, capable of self-fertilization, and lays eggs in gelatinous masses attached to submerged surfaces.

Life Style Summary

This snail is primarily herbivorous, feeding on algae, detritus, and plant material. It is often seen grazing on the surfaces of submerged plants and rocks. It is active throughout the year, although its activity levels decrease during colder months. In captivity, it can be fed a diet of blanched vegetables, algae wafers, and other plant-based foods. The species is known for its respiratory adaptability, using both a rudimentary lung for atmospheric breathing and gills for aquatic respiration.

Interesting Fact

The Great Pond Snail has been extensively studied for its learning and memory capabilities. It is one of the few invertebrates known to exhibit conditioned reflexes, making it a valuable model organism in neuroscience research. Scientists have discovered that it can be trained to respond to stimuli, providing insights into the basic principles of learning and memory.
