Habitat Description

The Penguin Tetra (Thayeria boehlkei) is native to the slow-moving tributaries and streams of the Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in Peru and Brazil. These waters are often clear to slightly turbid, with a moderate current and a mix of submerged vegetation and leaf litter that provides shelter and feeding grounds. The natural habitat is typically soft, slightly acidic to neutral, with sandy or muddy substrates.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Characidae and is known for its distinctive black stripe that runs from the gills to the base of the tail, giving it a penguin-like appearance. Penguin Tetras grow to an average size of about 5 cm (2 inches). They are egg scatterers and do not exhibit parental care, with the eggs hatching within 24 to 48 hours. As of 2024, the species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, with no significant threats to its population in the wild.

Life Style Summary

Penguin Tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that do best in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and prefer the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, larvae, and plant detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet that includes high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

The unique swimming posture of the Penguin Tetra, with a slight tilt upwards, is a natural adaptation that allows it to remain steady in flowing waters while searching for food. This tilted position also helps reduce predation risk by breaking its silhouette, making it less visible to predators in the wild.
