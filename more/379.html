Habitat Description

The Fourspotted Toothcarp (Phallichthys quadripunctatus) is native to the slow-moving freshwater rivers and streams of Central America, particularly in Costa Rica and Nicaragua. These fish are typically found in shallow waters with dense vegetation, including submerged roots and aquatic plants. The water in their natural habitat is warm, ranging from 22-28°C (72-82°F), slightly alkaline, and has a soft, sandy or muddy substrate.

They are characterized by four distinct dark spots on their sides. This species is not currently considered endangered, but their populations can be influenced by habitat degradation and water pollution. Fourspotted Toothcarps are livebearers, with females giving birth to fully formed, free-swimming fry.

Life Style Summary

Fourspotted Toothcarps are peaceful and social fish that prefer to live in small groups. They are active swimmers and often occupy the middle to upper levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and algae. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and daphnia. A well-planted aquarium with gentle water flow and a stable temperature is ideal for this species.
