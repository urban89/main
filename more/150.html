Habitat Description

The Three-lined Pencilfish (Nannostomus trifasciatus) is native to the slow-moving blackwater rivers and tributaries of the Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. These waters are typically acidic, stained dark with tannins from decaying plant matter, and have low mineral content. The natural habitat includes areas with dense vegetation and leaf litter, providing cover and feeding grounds for the fish.

Scientific Facts

The Three-lined Pencilfish belongs to the family Lebiasinidae, which includes several small, elongated freshwater species. Nannostomus trifasciatus typically grows to a length of about 5 cm (2 inches). It is characterized by three distinct dark stripes running horizontally along its body, which serve as camouflage in its natural environment. This species is not currently listed as endangered, but its populations are affected by habitat changes such as deforestation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Three-lined Pencilfish are peaceful, shoaling fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are most active in the middle to upper levels of the water column and prefer a well-planted aquarium with subdued lighting that mimics their natural environment. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia, brine shrimp, and mosquito larvae.

Interesting Fact

The Three-lined Pencilfish has a unique mode of swimming, often seen at an upward angle or hovering mid-water, which differentiates it from other schooling fish. This distinctive swimming behavior, combined with its striking pattern, makes it a popular choice among aquarists looking to add diversity to their community tanks.
