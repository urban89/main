Habitat Description

The Barred-tail Cory (Corydoras cochui) is native to the clear, slow-moving streams and small tributaries of the Rio Araguaia basin in Brazil, South America. These waters are typically shallow with a sandy or muddy substrate, surrounded by dense aquatic vegetation that provides cover and food. The natural habitat has soft, slightly acidic to neutral water with temperatures ranging from 22-26°C (72-79°F).

Scientific Facts

Barred-tail Corys belong to the family Callichthyidae, known for their small, armored catfish that are popular in the aquarium trade. Corydoras cochui is a small species, typically reaching a maximum length of 3.2 cm (1.3 inches). They are recognized by the distinctive dark vertical bars on their caudal (tail) fin, giving them their common name. This species is not currently considered endangered, but habitat loss due to deforestation and water pollution can pose a risk to their populations. Barred-tail Corys are egg scatterers and exhibit no parental care; the eggs typically hatch within 3 to 5 days.

Life Style Summary

Barred-tail Corys are peaceful, social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are bottom-dwellers, spending much of their time sifting through the substrate for food. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates, detritus, and plant material. In captivity, they do well on a diet of sinking pellets, wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp. A tank with soft substrate, plenty of hiding spots, and gentle water flow is ideal for their well-being.

Interesting Fact

The Barred-tail Cory is one of the smallest species of Corydoras, making them ideal for nano or small aquariums. They have a unique behavior of "blinking," achieved by rotating their eyes, which helps them remove debris from their corneas. Their distinctive tail markings and lively behavior make them a popular choice among aquarists who appreciate small, active fish.
