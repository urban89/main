Habitat Description

The Saulosi Mbuna (Pseudotropheus saulosi) is endemic to the rocky reefs of Lake Malawi, particularly around the Taiwan Reef. It inhabits shallow waters, typically at depths of 5 to 15 meters, where it can find shelter among the rocks. The water in these areas is clear, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a pH between 7.8 and 8.6.

Scientific Facts

This species, belonging to the family Cichlidae, exhibits striking sexual dimorphism. Males are bright blue with dark vertical bars, while females are yellow. They can reach up to 8 cm (3 inches) in length. As of 2024, Pseudotropheus saulosi is considered vulnerable due to overfishing and habitat loss, making conservation efforts crucial for its survival.

Life Style Summary

Saulosi Mbunas are active and territorial, preferring to live in rocky habitats that provide plenty of hiding spots. In the wild, they graze on algae growing on rocks, while in captivity they require a diet rich in vegetable matter, such as spirulina flakes, along with high-quality cichlid pellets. They are best kept in groups with a high female-to-male ratio to reduce aggression.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many other mbuna species, male Saulosi can change their color temporarily when stressed or during fights, displaying a duller or more intense blue to communicate their dominance or submission within their social hierarchy.
