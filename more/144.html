Habitat Description

The Pygmy Hatchetfish (Carnegiella myersi) is found in the slow-moving streams and shallow waters of the Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in Brazil, Peru, and Ecuador. These fish prefer soft, acidic waters with plenty of floating vegetation, providing cover and foraging grounds. Their habitat is typically characterized by dark, tannin-rich waters, a sandy or muddy substrate, and dense aquatic plants.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Gasteropelecidae and is one of the smallest hatchetfish, reaching a maximum size of about 2.5 cm (1 inch). Known for its hatchet-shaped body and ability to leap from the water, the Pygmy Hatchetfish is an egg scatterer that provides no parental care, with eggs hatching within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, it is listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, reflecting stable populations in its natural habitat.

Life Style Summary

They are peaceful and thrive in schools of six or more, staying near the water's surface. They are insectivores, feeding mainly on small insects and larvae that fall onto the water. In captivity, they require a diet of high-quality flake foods, micro-pellets, and live or frozen foods like fruit flies and mosquito larvae to mimic their natural feeding habits.

Interesting Fact

Pygmy Hatchetfish possess a unique flight mechanism; they can jump out of the water to escape predators or catch prey by rapidly flapping their pectoral fins, which act like wings. This adaptation allows them to evade danger and hunt insects just above the water surface, showcasing their remarkable agility and survival skills.
