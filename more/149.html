Habitat Description

The Coral Red Pencilfish (Nannostomus mortenthaleri) is native to the slow-moving streams and tributaries of the Peruvian Amazon, particularly in the Rio Nanay basin. These waters are typically clear and soft, with a slightly acidic pH due to the decomposition of organic matter from the surrounding rainforest. The fish are found in areas with dense aquatic vegetation, leaf litter, and submerged roots, which provide ample hiding spots and a source of food.

Scientific Facts

The Coral Red Pencilfish belongs to the family Lebiasinidae, a group known for their slender, elongated bodies. This species reaches an adult size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). Males are more vibrantly colored, displaying a deep red hue along their bodies, while females tend to be slightly paler. They are micro predators in the wild, feeding primarily on tiny invertebrates. Nannostomus mortenthaleri is not currently listed as endangered, but its natural habitats are under threat from deforestation and pollution, making conservation efforts important.

Life Style Summary

Coral Red Pencilfish are peaceful and prefer to live in small shoals of six or more. They are active swimmers and occupy the mid to upper levels of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, larvae, and zooplankton. In an aquarium setting, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and live or frozen foods like daphnia and brine shrimp. They require a well-planted tank with subdued lighting and gentle water flow to mimic their natural environment.

Interesting Fact

The Coral Red Pencilfish is named after its vibrant red coloration, which intensifies during mating displays. This species was first described in 2001 and is sometimes referred to as the "Peruvian Red Pencilfish." Despite being a relatively recent discovery, it has quickly become popular among aquarists for its striking appearance and peaceful nature.
