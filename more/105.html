Habitat Description

The Bleeding Heart Tetra (Hyphessobrycon erythrostigma) is native to the slow-moving rivers and streams of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Peru and Brazil. These fish thrive in warm, tropical waters with a temperature range of 22-28°C (72-82°F). Their natural habitat is heavily vegetated with soft, acidic water and a substrate composed of leaf litter and debris, which creates a dimly lit environment where they feel most comfortable.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the Characidae family and is known for the distinctive red spot on its body, resembling a "bleeding heart," from which its common name is derived. Bleeding Heart Tetras grow to an average size of 6 cm (2.4 inches) and are schooling fish, preferring groups of six or more. They are categorized as "Least Concern" by conservation status, indicating a stable population in their native range (2024).

Life Style Summary

These tetras are peaceful and best kept in community aquariums with other similarly sized, non-aggressive fish. They are active swimmers, typically occupying the middle layers of the tank. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, worms, and plant matter. In an aquarium setting, they are easy to feed and will accept a range of foods, including high-quality flakes, pellets, and live or frozen options such as bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

The red "bleeding heart" spot on these fish intensifies when they are excited or during courtship displays, serving as a communication signal to other members of their species.
