Habitat Description

The Pygmy Cory (Corydoras pygmaeus) is native to the freshwater rivers of South America, particularly in the Madeira River basin in Brazil. This species is commonly found in small, slow-moving tributaries with clear water and a sandy or muddy substrate. The natural environment is often densely vegetated with submerged plants and leaf litter, providing shelter and feeding grounds.

Scientific Facts

Pygmy Corys belong to the family Callichthyidae, known for their small size and armored bodies. Corydoras pygmaeus is one of the smallest corydoras species, reaching a maximum length of about 2.5 cm (1 inch). They are unique among corydoras due to their preference for swimming in the mid-water level rather than staying at the bottom. This species is currently not considered endangered, but it faces threats from habitat degradation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Pygmy Corys are peaceful and social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. Unlike most corydoras, they are active swimmers and often form small shoals in the middle of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and detritus. In an aquarium setting, they do well on a diet of high-quality micro-pellets, flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. A tank with soft substrate, gentle water flow, and plenty of hiding places is ideal for them.

Interesting Fact

Pygmy Corys are known for their playful behavior and unique social interactions. They often engage in synchronized swimming, moving together in small groups throughout the tank. This behavior makes them a lively and entertaining addition to community aquariums.
