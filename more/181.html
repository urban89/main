Habitat Description

The Chipokee Cichlid is native to Lake Malawi, where it inhabits rocky shorelines and sandy bottoms. These environments provide cover and hunting grounds for this aggressive species. The water in their natural habitat is clear and well-oxygenated, with temperatures ranging from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the Cichlidae family. Males of the Chipokee Cichlid grow to about 15 cm (6 inches), while females remain slightly smaller. Males exhibit strong territorial behavior, especially during breeding. As of (2024), the species is classified as "Least Concern" in the wild. Like many cichlids, it is a mouthbrooder, with females incubating their eggs in their mouths for protection.

Life Style Summary

The Chipokee Cichlid is highly territorial, especially during breeding seasons. Males will aggressively defend their area against other fish. In the wild, they feed primarily on smaller fish and invertebrates. In captivity, they require a high-protein diet, including pellets, live food, and frozen options. They are best housed in large tanks with plenty of rock structures to provide hiding spaces.

Interesting Fact

This species displays extreme sexual dimorphism, with males exhibiting striking blue and black coloration while females are a yellowish-orange. The intense difference in appearance between males and females helps them quickly identify each other during breeding.
