Habitat Description

The Hyphessobrycon rosaceus is native to the river systems of the Guiana Shield in South America, particularly in Suriname and Guyana. This species thrives in slow-moving, densely vegetated blackwater streams and tributaries with soft, acidic water. The natural habitat is typically shaded by overhanging trees, resulting in limited light penetration and dark, tannin-stained water.

Scientific Facts

Rosy Tetras are part of the family Characidae, growing up to 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. Males are known for their deep pink to reddish coloration, while females display lighter shades. This species is considered "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024), with stable populations in its native habitat. They are egg scatterers that provide no care for their offspring, with eggs typically hatching within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful, active swimmers that prefer to live in schools of six or more. They occupy the middle to upper levels of the water column and are compatible with other small, non-aggressive species. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, worms, and plant material. In captivity, they do well on a balanced diet of flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

Rosy Tetras have a unique habit of engaging in fin displays, where males use their elongated dorsal and anal fins to signal dominance or court potential mates. This behavior is most noticeable in the presence of other males or during breeding periods, adding an extra layer of visual interest to community aquariums.
