Habitat Description

The Flag Cichlid (Mesonauta festivus), also known as the Festivum Cichlid, is native to the slow-moving rivers, streams, and floodplain lakes of the Amazon and Orinoco basins in South America. These waters are typically warm, soft, and slightly acidic, with dense vegetation and submerged roots providing ample hiding spots.

Scientific Facts

Mesonauta festivus belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. First described in 1840, this species is known for the distinct black diagonal stripe running from its mouth to its dorsal fin. The species is not considered endangered (2024), though habitat loss due to deforestation poses a potential threat in the future.

Life Style Summary

Flag Cichlids are generally peaceful and thrive in aquariums with ample plants and hiding spaces. In the wild, they are omnivorous, feeding on small invertebrates, plant matter, and detritus. In captivity, they can be fed a varied diet of high-quality flakes, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or bloodworms. They are known to form monogamous pairs and exhibit strong parental care.

Interesting Fact

During breeding, Flag Cichlids become highly territorial and protective. They are known to guard their fry closely, even going as far as "herding" the young fish by using subtle fin movements to keep them close.
