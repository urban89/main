Habitat Description

Native to the rocky coastlines of Lake Tanganyika in East Africa, this species thrives in the shallow, clear waters along the lake's steep rocky slopes. It typically inhabits depths ranging from 3 to 25 meters, where it can find ample hiding places and establish breeding territories. The water is clear, with temperatures between 23-27°C (73-81°F) and a pH ranging from 8.0 to 9.0.

Scientific Facts

Neolamprologus brichardi belongs to the family Cichlidae and is recognized by its slender body, elongated lyre-shaped caudal fin, and the distinctive white stripe running from the eye to the gill cover. This species can grow up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length. As of 2024, it is not considered endangered, but local populations could be affected by habitat degradation and the aquarium trade.

Life Style Summary

These fish are known for their complex social structures and cooperative breeding behavior. They form colonies with a dominant breeding pair and multiple generations of offspring. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and zooplankton. In aquariums, they thrive in rocky setups that replicate their natural environment, with a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, supplemented with live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and daphnia. They can be territorial, especially during breeding, and should be housed with other Tanganyikan species that share similar water requirements.

Interesting Fact

This species is often referred to as the “Princess of Burundi” due to its graceful appearance and unique social behavior. It exhibits a fascinating brood care system where older offspring assist their parents in protecting and raising the younger fry, creating a complex social hierarchy rarely seen in other fish species.
