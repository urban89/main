Habitat Description

The Brevis Shelldweller (Neolamprologus brevis) is native to the rocky, sandy shores of Lake Tanganyika in East Africa. These fish inhabit the sandy bottoms where they occupy empty snail shells, which they use as both shelter and breeding sites. The water in Lake Tanganyika is alkaline and hard, with a high mineral content, providing the ideal environment for these unique cichlids.

Scientific Facts

Neolamprologus brevis belongs to the family Cichlidae, a diverse group of freshwater fish known for their complex behaviors. This species grows to a maximum size of about 6 cm (2.4 inches). The Brevis Shelldweller is not considered endangered and has a stable population in the wild. It is a shell-dwelling cichlid, displaying fascinating behaviors related to territory and reproduction within their chosen shells.

Life Style Summary

Brevis Shelldwellers are relatively solitary but can be kept in pairs or small groups in a spacious tank. They are territorial, especially around their shells, but generally peaceful toward other species that do not invade their space. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and organic detritus. In an aquarium setting, they thrive on a diet of high-quality pellets, flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

Brevis Shelldwellers have the unique ability to completely retreat into their shells, sealing the entrance with their bodies to protect themselves from predators. This species is one of the smallest shell-dwelling cichlids, and their compact size and fascinating behavior make them a popular choice among aquarists interested in biotope aquariums.
