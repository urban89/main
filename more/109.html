Habitat Description

The Buenos Aires Tetra (Hyphessobrycon anisitsi) originates from the freshwater rivers and streams of South America, particularly in the La Plata Basin, which spans across Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina. These fish are typically found in clear to slightly turbid waters with moderate to strong currents. The habitat is often characterized by sandy or gravel substrates and abundant aquatic vegetation, which provides shelter and foraging grounds.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Characidae, Buenos Aires Tetras are hardy, medium-sized fish that grow to approximately 7 cm (2.75 inches) in length. They are known for their silver body with a distinctive red or orange-tinted fin. Due to their robust nature, they are one of the more resilient tetra species. As of now, the conservation status of this species is "Least Concern," with stable populations reported in their native regions (2024). These fish are egg scatterers and provide no parental care, with eggs typically hatching within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These fish are active and fast swimmers, often seen darting around the tank's middle to lower levels. They are best kept in schools of six or more to prevent aggressive behavior. In the wild, their diet includes small insects, worms, and plant matter. In an aquarium setting, they are omnivorous and will accept a wide range of foods, including flakes, pellets, and live or frozen options such as bloodworms and daphnia. Due to their energetic nature, they require a spacious tank with plenty of swimming room.

Interesting Fact

Buenos Aires Tetras are known for their voracious appetite for aquatic plants, making them notorious among aquarists who maintain planted tanks. They have been observed nibbling on softer plant leaves, so it is advisable to keep them with hardier plant species or in tanks without plants.
