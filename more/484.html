Habitat Description

The Raja Betta (Betta raja) is native to the peat swamps and slow-moving streams of Sumatra, Indonesia. These waters are typically acidic, with a pH ranging from 4.0 to 6.5, and are stained dark by tannins released from decaying organic matter. The natural habitat is densely vegetated, providing ample cover and creating a dimly lit environment. The water is warm, usually around 24-28°C (75-82°F), which is ideal for the species.

Scientific Facts

Raja Bettas belong to the family Osphronemidae, which includes other well-known Betta species. They are labyrinth fish, meaning they possess a specialized organ that allows them to breathe atmospheric air, enabling them to survive in low-oxygen environments. Betta raja typically grows to about 5-6 cm (2-2.4 inches) in length. Males are known for their elaborate fins and vibrant colors, which are used to attract females during breeding. The species is currently not listed as endangered, but its natural habitat is increasingly threatened by deforestation and habitat destruction in Sumatra.

Life Style Summary

Raja Bettas are relatively peaceful compared to other Betta species, though males can be territorial. They are best kept in a species-only tank or with other non-aggressive species. In the wild, they feed on small insects, larvae, and other invertebrates. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality Betta pellets, live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp. They thrive in a well-planted aquarium with plenty of hiding spots and prefer slightly acidic, soft water conditions.

Interesting Fact

Raja Bettas are unique among Betta species for their relatively peaceful nature and the stunning metallic sheen that can be seen on their bodies under the right lighting conditions. This species is also known for its bubble-nesting behavior, where the male builds a nest of bubbles at the water's surface to protect the eggs after spawning.
