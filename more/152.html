Habitat Description

Native to the rivers and streams of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil and Peru, Metynnis argenteus thrives in fast-flowing, oxygen-rich waters with sandy or rocky substrates. These areas are often shaded by overhanging vegetation, which provides natural cover and maintains the slightly acidic water conditions they prefer.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Characidae, this species is often referred to as the Silver Dollar due to its disc-shaped body and silvery sheen, resembling a coin. Another similar species, Metynnis hypsauchen, also shares the common name "Silver Dollar." These fish can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. As of 2024, they are listed as Least Concern, with stable populations in the wild, although habitat degradation remains a potential threat.

Life Style Summary

Silver Dollars are schooling fish and prefer to live in groups of at least five or more to feel secure. They are active swimmers, occupying the middle to upper levels of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed primarily on plant matter, such as fruits, leaves, and algae. In aquariums, they require a diet rich in plant-based foods, such as vegetable flakes, spirulina, and occasional fresh vegetables like spinach or lettuce.

Interesting Fact

Silver Dollars are known to be voracious herbivores, often consuming large amounts of aquatic vegetation in their habitat. This behavior helps maintain the balance of their ecosystem by controlling plant growth and contributing to nutrient cycling within their native waters.
