Habitat Description

The Black Neon Tetra (Hyphessobrycon herbertaxelrodi) is native to the freshwater rivers and streams of South America, particularly in the Paraguay and Taquari river basins in Brazil. These fish are found in slow-moving waters with a sandy or muddy substrate and are often surrounded by dense aquatic vegetation. The natural habitat typically features soft, acidic water with a dark tint caused by tannins from decaying organic matter.

Scientific Facts

Black Neon Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are small, schooling fish that grow to about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. They are characterized by a black longitudinal stripe bordered by a shimmering white line along their body. This species is known for its resilience and adaptability to a range of water conditions. Hyphessobrycon herbertaxelrodi is not currently listed as endangered in the wild, although their habitats are affected by deforestation and pollution.

Life Style Summary

Black Neon Tetras are peaceful and thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers that occupy the middle levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates, plant matter, and detritus. In an aquarium, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and daphnia. They prefer a well-planted tank with subdued lighting that mimics their natural habitat.

Interesting Fact

The Black Neon Tetra was named in honor of Dr. Herbert R. Axelrod ('herbertaxelrodi''), a renowned ichthyologist and publisher who made significant contributions to aquarium fish literature.
