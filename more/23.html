Habitat Description

The Dwarf Cory (Corydoras hastatus) is native to the freshwater rivers and floodplains of South America, specifically in Brazil, Argentina, and Paraguay. This species is typically found in slow-moving, shallow waters with soft, sandy or muddy substrates. The natural environment is often densely vegetated with aquatic plants, providing shelter and a rich source of food in the form of small invertebrates and detritus.

Scientific Facts

The Dwarf Cory belongs to the family Callichthyidae, a group of small, armored catfish. Corydoras hastatus is among the smallest of its genus, reaching a maximum size of about 2.5 cm (1 inch). Unlike many other corydoras, this species is known for its unique behavior of swimming mid-water in schools rather than staying on the substrate. The species is not currently considered endangered, but habitat degradation and pollution could impact wild populations over time.

Life Style Summary

Dwarf Corys are peaceful, social fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. They are active during the day and display unique swimming behaviors, often schooling in the middle of the water column, unlike most bottom-dwelling corydoras. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant matter, and detritus. In an aquarium setting, they thrive on a diet of high-quality sinking pellets, wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and bloodworms. A tank with soft substrate and plenty of plants is ideal for this species.

Interesting Fact

The Dwarf Cory is one of the few corydoras species that does not always adhere to bottom-dwelling behavior. They often swim in mid-water shoals, mimicking the behavior of small tetras. This unique trait, along with their small size and peaceful nature, makes them a fascinating addition to community aquariums.
