Habitat Description

The Chitala ornata is native to the slow-moving rivers, lakes, and swamps of Southeast Asia, including the Chao Phraya and Mekong river basins in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam. It thrives in freshwater environments with dense vegetation, submerged roots, and muddy substrates. These waters are often murky with low visibility, providing cover for this ambush predator.

Scientific Facts

Clown Knifefish are members of the family Notopteridae, known for their distinctive elongated body and knife-like shape. They can grow up to 100 cm (39 inches) in length, although most specimens in captivity reach about 50-60 cm (20-24 inches). The species is considered "Least Concern" by the IUCN Red List (2024), but local populations may be threatened by overfishing and habitat loss. Chitala ornata are nocturnal hunters, primarily feeding on smaller fish and invertebrates.

Life Style Summary

Solitary and territorial, preferring to inhabit areas with plenty of hiding spots such as caves, dense plants, or driftwood. They are primarily active at night and exhibit a unique undulating swimming pattern, moving both forward and backward with ease. In captivity, they require a varied diet that includes live or frozen foods like shrimp, fish fillets, and worms. Due to their size and predatory nature, they are best suited for large, species-specific aquariums.

Interesting Fact

The Clown Knifefish is known for its unique ability to navigate both forwards and backwards with remarkable agility, using its long, continuous anal fin. This adaptability is enhanced by its lateral line system, which detects water movements and helps the fish hunt in low-light conditions. In its native habitat, it is also prized as a food fish and is a popular species for sport fishing in Southeast Asia.
