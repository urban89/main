Habitat Description

The Brook Stickleback is commonly found in cool, slow-moving streams, small rivers, and ponds across North America, particularly in the northern United States and Canada. These habitats typically feature clear water with sandy or muddy bottoms and abundant aquatic vegetation, which provides cover and breeding sites. The water is usually soft to moderately hard, with temperatures ranging from 10°C to 22°C (50°F to 72°F).

Scientific Facts

A member of the Gasterosteidae family, this small fish reaches about 5 to 7 cm (2 to 2.8 inches) in length. It is known for its unique body armor of bony plates and its ability to thrive in a variety of freshwater habitats, from pristine streams to polluted waters. As of 2024, it is listed as Least Concern, but localized threats, such as habitat loss and water pollution, could impact specific populations. The Brook Stickleback is a nest builder, with males constructing nests and guarding the eggs until they hatch.

Life Style Summary

This species is known for its solitary and territorial behavior outside the breeding season. Males are particularly aggressive during breeding, fiercely guarding their nests. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, crustaceans, and insect larvae. In captivity, they require a diet rich in live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp to maintain their health and vibrant behavior.

Interesting Fact

Brook Sticklebacks have a unique physiological adaptation that allows them to survive in low-oxygen environments, such as stagnant ponds or streams. This adaptation, combined with their ability to tolerate a wide range of water conditions, has enabled them to thrive in various habitats, including those impacted by human activities.
