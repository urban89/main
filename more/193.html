Habitat Description

The Bumblebee Cichlid (Pseudotropheus crabro) is native to the rocky shores of Lake Malawi in East Africa. It typically inhabits shallow waters around caves and crevices, where it can find shelter and feeding opportunities. The water in these regions is clear, with a temperature range of 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a pH between 7.8 and 8.6.

Scientific Facts

This cichlid, part of the family Cichlidae, can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. It is known for its striking yellow and black vertical stripes, resembling a bumblebee, which provides effective camouflage in its natural habitat. Pseudotropheus crabro is also known for its symbiotic relationship with the catfish species Bagrus meridionalis, helping to clean parasites from the catfish’s skin. The population is currently stable in Lake Malawi, but habitat changes and overfishing could pose future risks (2024).

Life Style Summary

This species is omnivorous and opportunistic, feeding on algae, invertebrates, and small fish. In aquariums, they require a varied diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, vegetables, and occasional live or frozen foods. They are territorial and aggressive, especially during breeding, and should be kept with other robust species in large tanks with plenty of hiding spaces among rocks.

Interesting Fact

Bumblebee Cichlids can change their coloration dramatically based on their mood or environment, shifting from their typical yellow and black pattern to a darker, almost black coloration. This ability allows them to blend into different environments or signal their status to other fish.
