Habitat Description

Epalzeorhynchos frenatum, commonly known as the Rainbow Shark, originates from freshwater rivers, streams, and floodplains in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia. These habitats are typically fast-moving with rocky substrates and dense vegetation. Water temperatures in their natural environment range from 22°C to 26°C (72°F to 79°F), with a slightly acidic to neutral pH.

Scientific Facts

Rainbow Sharks belong to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. Known for their striking black bodies with bright red or orange fins, these fish are popular in aquariums. The species is currently listed as "Least Concern" in the wild (2024), but habitat destruction poses a potential risk to its population. Despite their "shark" name, they are not related to marine sharks.

Life Style Summary

Rainbow Sharks are semi-aggressive and territorial, especially towards other bottom-dwelling species or members of their own kind. In the wild, they are omnivorous, feeding on algae, plant matter, and small invertebrates. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of algae wafers, vegetable matter, and protein-based foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp. They prefer aquariums with ample hiding spots and caves.

Interesting Fact

Despite their solitary nature in aquariums, in the wild, juvenile Rainbow Sharks live in groups, only becoming territorial as they mature. This territorial behavior intensifies in smaller aquariums, which is why they are best suited for larger tanks with plenty of space to minimize aggression.
