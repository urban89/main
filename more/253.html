Habitat Description

The Epalzeorhynchos bicolor is native to the freshwater basins of Thailand, particularly the Chao Phraya River. It inhabits clear, fast-flowing rivers and streams with rocky or sandy substrates. These environments are characterized by dense aquatic vegetation and submerged roots that provide shelter. The water is usually warm, ranging from 22-26°C (72-79°F), and has a slightly acidic to neutral pH.

Scientific Facts

The Red-tailed Black Shark belongs to the family Cyprinidae and typically reaches a length of up to 15 cm (6 inches). Known for its striking black body and vivid red tail, this species is a popular choice among aquarium enthusiasts. As of 2024, it is classified as "Critically Endangered" by the IUCN due to habitat destruction and overfishing in its native range. This species is an egg-layer that requires specific conditions to breed in captivity, and its eggs usually hatch in 2 to 3 days.

Life Style Summary

The Red-tailed Black Shark is a solitary and territorial fish that prefers to occupy the bottom levels of the aquarium. It can display aggressive behavior towards its own species and similar-looking fish. In its natural habitat, it feeds on algae, small invertebrates, and organic matter. In captivity, it does well on a diet of sinking pellets, algae wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or bloodworms. It thrives in a well-oxygenated tank with plenty of hiding spots.

Interesting Fact

The species is known for its color-changing ability, which can be influenced by mood, health, and water conditions. When stressed or ill, the vibrant red tail can lose its color, serving as an indicator for aquarists to assess the fish’s environment and health.
