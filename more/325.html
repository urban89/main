Habitat Description

The Poropanchax normani is found in the shallow, slow-moving waters of West Africa, particularly in Nigeria and Cameroon. These environments are often densely vegetated with submerged plants and have soft, slightly acidic to neutral water. The species thrives in shaded areas, where light penetration is low, and the water remains clear, creating an ideal habitat for this small fish.

Scientific Facts

Norman's Lampeye is a member of the family Poeciliidae and reaches a size of about 3 cm (1.2 inches). It is known for its distinctive iridescent blue eyes that reflect light, giving them a "lampeye" appearance. As of 2024, this species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to its stable populations in the wild. The fish are egg scatterers, with eggs hatching in 10 to 14 days, depending on the water temperature.

Life Style Summary

These fish are schooling and should be kept in groups of six or more. They prefer the middle to upper levels of the water column, where they can actively swim. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects and plankton. In captivity, they do well on a varied diet of flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or micro-worms. They thrive in well-planted tanks with gentle water flow.

Interesting Fact

This species is named after the American ichthyologist J. R. Norman, who first described it in 1928. Norman's Lampeye has an exceptional ability to detect polarized light, which helps them locate prey and navigate their environment, a trait that sets them apart from many other freshwater fish.
