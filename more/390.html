Habitat Description

The Flag Tetra (Hyphessobrycon heterorhabdus) is native to the lower Amazon River basin in Brazil, particularly in slow-moving streams, rivers, and flooded forest areas. These waters are typically soft and slightly acidic, with a dense presence of aquatic plants and submerged wood. The natural habitat of the Flag Tetra is characterized by shaded environments with minimal light penetration due to the thick overhead canopy of the rainforest.

Scientific Facts

Flag Tetras belong to the family Characidae. They are small, reaching about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length, and are distinguished by a horizontal red stripe running along the middle of their body, complemented by a black stripe below it. They are schooling fish and thrive in groups. The conservation status of Flag Tetras is currently not considered threatened in the wild, but habitat degradation and pollution can impact their populations.

Life Style Summary

Flag Tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of at least six or more. They are active swimmers, spending much of their time in the middle levels of the aquarium. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant matter, and detritus. In captivity, they do well on a diet of flake foods, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Flag Tetras have a unique behavior known as "mock spawning," where they exhibit spawning behavior without actual reproduction. This social activity strengthens bonds within the group and is often observed in the presence of other tetras. While this behavior can be seen in their natural habitat, it is also commonly observed in captivity, especially in densely planted aquariums that replicate their natural environment.
