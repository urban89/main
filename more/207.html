Habitat Description

Found in the shallow waters of Lake Kyoga and Lake Nawampasa in Uganda, this species inhabits areas with sandy and rocky substrates. It prefers the intermediate zones where rocky structures provide hiding spots and open areas allow for foraging. The water is typically clear, with temperatures between 22-26°C (72-79°F) and a pH ranging from 7.5 to 8.5.

Scientific Facts

Astatotilapia latifasciata belongs to the family Cichlidae and is characterized by its striking vertical black and white stripes, which are more pronounced in males. They can grow up to 12 cm (4.7 inches) in length. As of 2024, this species is considered vulnerable in the wild due to habitat loss and competition with invasive species. Conservation efforts are necessary to protect its natural populations.

Life Style Summary

These cichlids are social and moderately aggressive, especially during breeding. They thrive in aquariums with plenty of hiding spots and open swimming areas. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and detritus. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, supplemented with live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms. They should be housed with other robust species to manage aggression.

Interesting Fact

Males of this species exhibit intense coloration during the breeding season, with bright red and yellow hues on their body and fins. This dramatic color change, combined with their aggressive courtship displays, helps them attract females and establish dominance within their territory.
