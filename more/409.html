Habitat Description

The Javanese Ricefish (Oryzias javanicus) is found in the freshwater and brackish waters of Southeast Asia, particularly in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore. They inhabit shallow, slow-moving bodies of water such as rice paddies, ditches, and estuaries. These habitats often have dense aquatic vegetation and a soft substrate, with water that can range from fresh to slightly brackish, typically warm, between 22-28°C (72-82°F).

Scientific Facts

Javanese Ricefish belong to the family Adrianichthyidae, which includes small, adaptable fish known for their tolerance to a variety of environmental conditions. Oryzias javanicus reaches a maximum size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They exhibit a transparent or slightly silvery body color with a subtle yellow hue, particularly around the fins. This species is not currently considered endangered, but habitat loss due to agricultural expansion and pollution may affect local populations. They are egg layers, with females depositing adhesive eggs on aquatic plants.

Life Style Summary

Javanese Ricefish are peaceful and social fish, often found in small groups. They are surface feeders, consuming small insects, zooplankton, and algae. In an aquarium setting, they do well on a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and live or frozen foods such as daphnia and brine shrimp. They prefer a well-planted tank with gentle water flow and can tolerate a range of water salinities, making them suitable for both freshwater and slightly brackish aquariums.

Interesting Fact

Javanese Ricefish are known for their resilience and ability to thrive in a wide range of water conditions, including brackish environments. Their adaptability has made them popular in scientific research as a model organism for studying environmental tolerance and evolutionary biology. Additionally, they are one of the few species that can comfortably live in both freshwater and brackish setups, offering unique versatility for aquarists.
