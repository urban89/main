Habitat Description

The Flame Tetra (Hyphessobrycon flammeus) is native to the coastal rivers of southeastern Brazil, particularly the Rio de Janeiro region. These fish thrive in slow-moving streams and rivers that are heavily shaded by overhanging vegetation. The water in their natural habitat is typically soft and slightly acidic, with a temperature range of 22-28°C (72-82°F). The substrate is often composed of fine sand and leaf litter, providing a rich environment for microorganisms, which form a significant part of their diet.

Scientific Facts

Hyphessobrycon flammeus belongs to the family Characidae. This species is recognized for its fiery red coloration, particularly in males, which intensifies during breeding. Flame Tetras grow to a maximum size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They are schooling fish and prefer to stay in groups, which enhances their coloration and reduces stress. In terms of conservation, the Flame Tetra's natural habitats have been significantly impacted by urbanization and pollution, placing the species under threat in the wild. However, they are not currently listed as endangered due to their widespread presence in the aquarium trade.

Life Style Summary

Flame Tetras are peaceful and thrive in a community aquarium setting. They are active swimmers and are best kept in schools of six or more to promote natural behavior and reduce stress. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, larvae, and other tiny invertebrates. In captivity, they adapt well to a varied diet that includes high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

The Flame Tetra is known for its dynamic color change during courtship and breeding, where males display an even more intense red hue to attract females.
