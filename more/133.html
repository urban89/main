Habitat Description

The Serpae Tetra (Hyphessobrycon serpae) is native to the slow-moving rivers, streams, and floodplains of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil, Paraguay, and northern Argentina. These fish thrive in environments with dense vegetation, submerged roots, and fallen leaves that provide shelter and feeding grounds. The water in their natural habitat is typically soft and slightly acidic to neutral, with a sandy or muddy substrate.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Characidae and grows to an average size of about 4 cm (1.6 inches). Known for their distinct red color and black spot behind the gills, they are egg scatterers, providing no parental care. The eggs hatch within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, the species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in its native range with no immediate threats.

Life Style Summary

Serpae Tetras are schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. They are active and prefer the middle levels of the water column. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, crustaceans, and plant material. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet that includes flake foods, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

These tetras exhibit a unique behavior known as "fin-nipping," where they occasionally nip the fins of slower-moving tank mates. This behavior is part of their natural instinct, especially in smaller tanks, and can be minimized by keeping them in larger groups to disperse any aggression.
