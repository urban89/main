Habitat Description

The Green Neon Tetra (Paracheirodon simulans) is native to the Rio Negro and Orinoco River basins in South America, particularly in Brazil and Colombia. These fish inhabit slow-moving, tannin-rich waters in dense forest areas. Their natural environment features soft, acidic water with a pH ranging from 4.0 to 6.5, and temperatures between 23°C to 27°C (73°F to 81°F). The rivers are often shaded by overhanging vegetation, resulting in low light conditions.

Scientific Facts

Green Neon Tetras are members of the family Characidae, closely related to the more common Neon Tetra. They grow to about 2.5 cm (1 inch) in length and are known for their iridescent blue-green coloration, which runs along the length of their body. This species is a schooling fish, often found in large groups in the wild. Paracheirodon simulans is considered of Least Concern by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in its natural habitat, although habitat degradation remains a potential threat.

Life Style Summary

Green Neon Tetras are peaceful, shoaling fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are highly active and prefer the middle to upper water columns of their environment. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, microcrustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen food such as brine shrimp or daphnia. They are best kept in a well-planted aquarium with dim lighting to replicate their natural environment.

Interesting Fact

Green Neon Tetras are often mistaken for Neon Tetras due to their similar appearance, but they have a more subtle greenish hue. This species was only discovered and described in 1963, much later than its more famous cousin. The unique reflective properties of their scales are due to microscopic layers that refract light, giving them their vibrant, shimmering appearance that makes them a prized addition to any planted aquarium.
