Habitat Description

The Clown Barb (Barbodes everetti) is native to the rivers and streams of Southeast Asia, particularly in Borneo and Sumatra. These fish thrive in slow to moderately flowing waters with sandy or rocky substrates, dense vegetation, and plenty of submerged branches and leaf litter. The water is typically soft and slightly acidic to neutral, providing a natural environment rich in organic material.

Scientific Facts

Barbodes everetti belongs to the family Cyprinidae, known for their diverse and vibrant freshwater species. Clown Barbs can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length, making them one of the larger barb species. They are easily recognized by their bright orange body with distinct black vertical stripes. Clown Barbs are egg scatterers that show no parental care, with the eggs often laid among plants. Currently, this species is not endangered, although habitat changes pose potential future threats.

Life Style Summary

Clown Barbs are active and social shoaling fish that do best in groups of six or more. They are peaceful but require plenty of swimming space due to their size and activity level. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

Clown Barbs are known for their vibrant colors, which can intensify during breeding or in well-maintained environments. Their striking appearance and lively nature make them a popular choice among aquarists looking to add dynamic movement and color to their aquariums.
