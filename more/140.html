Habitat Description

The Yellow-tailed Congo Tetra (Alestopetersius caudalis) is native to the slow-flowing rivers and streams of the Congo River Basin in Central Africa. These fish are typically found in freshwater environments with soft to moderately hard water, slightly acidic to neutral pH levels, and abundant aquatic vegetation. The substrate in their habitat is usually sandy or muddy, providing ample food and shelter.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Alestidae and reaches a length of up to 6 cm (2.4 inches). Known for its yellow-tipped tail fin and iridescent body, it is an egg scatterer that does not exhibit parental care. The eggs hatch within 36 to 48 hours. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, with stable populations in the wild.

Life Style Summary

Yellow-tailed Congo Tetras are schooling fish that thrive in groups of six or more, where they display more vibrant colors and natural behavior. They are active swimmers, preferring the middle to upper levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, larvae, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The species displays a fascinating behavior known as "flashing," where they tilt their bodies to reflect light off their scales, creating a sparkling effect. This behavior is thought to play a role in communication and courtship.
