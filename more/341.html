Habitat Description

The Trichopsis pumila is found in the slow-moving, heavily vegetated waters of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Cambodia, and Vietnam. These environments are typically shallow, warm, and rich in organic material. The water is often stained dark by tannins from decaying leaves, with a soft, slightly acidic to neutral pH, providing plenty of hiding spots and a subdued, low-light environment.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Osphronemidae and reaches a size of about 4 cm (1.6 inches). The Pygmy Gourami is known for its ability to produce sound, a trait that is unique among gouramis, using specialized pectoral fins. It is currently listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024) due to its stable populations in the wild. They are bubble nest builders, with males constructing nests at the water surface and providing care for the eggs until they hatch within 24 to 48 hours.

Life Style Summary

Pygmy Gouramis are peaceful and prefer living in groups, although they can also be kept in pairs. They tend to occupy the middle and upper levels of the aquarium and are relatively shy compared to other fish. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, larvae, and plankton. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or mosquito larvae.

Interesting Fact

The Pygmy Gourami is known for its ability to breathe atmospheric air, thanks to a specialized labyrinth organ. This adaptation allows it to survive in oxygen-poor waters where other fish might struggle, making it well-suited for environments with low oxygen levels.
