Habitat Description

The African Butterfly Cichlid (Anomalochromis thomasi) is native to the slow-moving streams and rivers of West Africa, specifically in Sierra Leone, Liberia, and Guinea. It inhabits shallow waters with a sandy or muddy substrate, often surrounded by dense aquatic vegetation. These environments provide ample hiding places and breeding grounds. The water in their natural habitat is soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging between 24-27°C (75-81°F).

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cichlidae, although it does not closely resemble typical cichlids in behavior or appearance. Anomalochromis thomasi reaches an average size of about 6 cm (2.4 inches). It is known for its peaceful temperament, making it a unique cichlid that can be kept in community tanks. The conservation status of this species is currently listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024), indicating it is not under immediate threat in its natural habitat.

Life Style Summary

African Butterfly Cichlids are monogamous and form strong pair bonds. They are known for their peaceful nature and can coexist with other non-aggressive fish species. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant matter, and detritus. In the aquarium, they do well on a diet of quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods. They are cave spawners and show parental care, guarding their eggs and fry attentively.

Interesting Fact

Despite being one of the smaller and lesser-known cichlids, the African Butterfly Cichlid displays a remarkable intelligence and adaptability. It can recognize individual human faces and may even form a bond with its owner, approaching the front of the tank when it senses a familiar presence.
