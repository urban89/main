Habitat Description

Found in the freshwater rivers and streams of Myanmar, particularly around the city of Mandalay, this species thrives in clear, fast-flowing water with a substrate composed of sand, gravel, and scattered rocks. The water in these habitats is typically soft to slightly hard, with a pH that ranges from slightly acidic to neutral, around 6.0 to 7.0, and temperatures ranging from 22°C to 26°C (72°F to 79°F).

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cyprinidae family, this species reaches an average length of about 5 to 7 cm (2 to 2.8 inches). As of 2024, it is not considered endangered, although habitat degradation and pollution could pose potential threats. The species exhibits dimorphism, with males displaying more vibrant colors than females, especially during breeding. Odessa Barbs are egg scatterers that show no parental care, and their eggs typically hatch within 24 to 48 hours.

Life Style Summary

Odessa Barbs are social and energetic, preferring to live in schools of six or more. They are active swimmers, often seen darting around the middle and lower levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet that includes high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The vibrant red and black coloration of males intensifies during courtship and spawning, serving as a visual signal to attract females. This dynamic color change is controlled by specialized chromatophores in their skin, which can rapidly expand or contract to alter the appearance, a fascinating adaptation for communication in their natural environment.
