Habitat Description

The Badis (Badis badis) is found in freshwater habitats across northern India, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Bhutan. It typically inhabits shallow, slow-flowing rivers, streams, and ponds with sandy or muddy substrates and ample vegetation. These environments often have a neutral to slightly acidic pH, with temperatures ranging from 20 to 26 degrees Celsius (68 to 79 degrees Fahrenheit).

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Badidae, this species is small, reaching up to 8 cm (3.1 inches) in length. Badis badis is known for its laterally compressed body and unique coloration, which changes depending on mood or breeding conditions. They are mouthbrooders, with the male guarding and caring for the eggs and fry. As of 2024, they are classified as Least Concern by the IUCN, with stable populations in their native habitats.

Life Style Summary

Badis badis is generally a solitary and territorial fish, particularly males, which prefer hiding spots and territory markers in aquariums. They are bottom dwellers, spending most of their time near the substrate or hiding among plants and decor. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, larvae, and other invertebrates. In captivity, they thrive on live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp, bloodworms, and daphnia, supplemented with high-quality pellets.

Interesting Fact

Badis badis has a unique ability to rapidly change color based on its mood, environmental conditions, or during breeding displays. This color change is facilitated by specialized cells in their skin called chromatophores, which expand or contract to reveal different colors and patterns.
