Habitat Description

The Blockhead Cichlid (Steatocranus casuarius) inhabits the fast-flowing, rocky streams and rapids of the Congo River in Central Africa. It prefers areas with numerous caves and crevices, where it can seek shelter and establish territories. The water in these environments is warm, ranging between 24-28°C (75-82°F), and slightly acidic to neutral.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the Cichlidae family and is distinctive due to the pronounced hump on its head, especially in males. It can reach up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length and has a reduced swim bladder, which aids in staying near the riverbed. It is currently classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024), indicating no immediate threat to its wild population.

Life Style Summary

These cichlids are bottom-dwellers, often found near rocky substrates where they use their strong fins to navigate the currents. They are territorial and form pairs during breeding. Their diet consists of small invertebrates and plant matter. In captivity, they do well with sinking pellets, frozen foods, and occasional vegetable supplements. They exhibit a unique "hopping" swimming style due to their specialized anatomy.

Interesting Fact

This fish exhibits a remarkable level of parental care. After laying eggs in caves or on flat surfaces, both parents take turns guarding and fanning them for oxygenation. This behavior extends to protecting and guiding the fry even after they hatch, showcasing complex and dedicated parental involvement uncommon among similar-sized cichlids.
