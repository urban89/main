Habitat Description

The Sixray Cory (Aspidoras pauciradiatus) is native to the freshwater rivers and streams of South America, particularly in Brazil's Rio Araguaia basin. They are commonly found in slow-moving, shallow waters with sandy or muddy substrates. Their natural habitat often includes areas with dense vegetation and submerged roots, providing cover and abundant food sources. The water in these regions is typically soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging from 22-28°C (72-82°F).

Scientific Facts

Sixray Corys belong to the family Callichthyidae, which includes small, armored catfish. Aspidoras pauciradiatus is a small species, usually growing up to 3.5 cm (1.4 inches) in length. They are named for the distinctive six rays found in their dorsal fin. This species is currently not considered endangered, but habitat degradation and pollution could threaten their populations. Like other Corydoras, they are egg scatterers, with the female depositing eggs on submerged surfaces, and the eggs hatching in about 3 to 5 days.

Life Style Summary

Sixray Corys are peaceful and social fish that thrive in small groups of six or more. They are active bottom-dwellers and spend most of their time foraging along the substrate for food. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality sinking pellets, wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and bloodworms. A well-planted tank with a soft substrate and gentle water flow is ideal for this species.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many other Corydoras species, Sixray Corys are known for their small size and slender bodies, making them ideal for nano aquariums. They are also unique in their ability to "blink" by rotating their eyes, which is actually a way to help clear debris from their corneas.
