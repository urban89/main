Habitat Description

The Bucktooth Tetra (Exodon paradoxus) inhabits the fast-flowing rivers and streams of the Amazon Basin in Brazil and Guyana. They are often found in areas with rocky substrates, submerged branches, and dense aquatic vegetation. The water in their natural habitat is typically clear to slightly murky, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a slightly acidic to neutral pH.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Characidae and is unique for its highly specialized dentition, featuring two prominent teeth that give it its common name. Bucktooth Tetras grow to about 7.5 cm (3 inches) in length and are known for their aggressive behavior, particularly towards other fish. The species is listed as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status, with a stable population in the wild (2024). Unlike many tetras, they are known to be fin nippers and may require species-only tanks.

Life Style Summary

They are active and aggressive schooling fish that require ample swimming space and a secure lid on the tank due to their jumping tendencies. They thrive in groups of ten or more to prevent intra-species aggression. In their natural environment, they feed on insects, small fish, and other invertebrates. In captivity, they should be fed a varied diet that includes high-protein foods such as live or frozen bloodworms, brine shrimp, and chopped fish.

Interesting Fact

Bucktooth Tetras display a unique hunting technique: they often form a loose group and target other fish's fins, nipping at them to consume the flesh. This unusual behavior, known as "scale-eating," is relatively rare among freshwater fish, making them a distinctive species in the aquarium trade.
