Habitat Description

The Salt and Pepper Cory (Corydoras habrosus) is native to the slow-moving rivers and streams of Venezuela and Colombia, particularly in the Orinoco River basin. These waters are typically shallow with a sandy or fine gravel substrate, often surrounded by dense aquatic vegetation. The natural habitat has soft, slightly acidic to neutral water, with temperatures ranging from 22-26°C (72-79°F).

Scientific Facts

Salt and Pepper Corys belong to the family Callichthyidae, a group of small, armored catfish. Corydoras habrosus is one of the smaller Corydoras species, reaching a maximum size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They are recognized by their light-colored body covered in black spots, which resembles a salt and pepper pattern. This species is not currently considered endangered, but it can be affected by habitat degradation and water pollution in its native range. Salt and Pepper Corys are egg scatterers, with eggs typically hatching within 3 to 5 days.

Life Style Summary

Salt and Pepper Corys are peaceful, social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active bottom-dwellers, often seen sifting through the substrate in search of food. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates, detritus, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of sinking pellets, wafers, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and daphnia. A well-planted tank with a soft substrate and gentle water flow is ideal for this species.

Interesting Fact

The Salt and Pepper Cory is known for its curious and active nature. Unlike many other bottom-dwelling species, they often swim up and down the glass of the aquarium, making them a lively addition to a community tank. Their small size and unique pattern make them particularly well-suited for nano or small aquariums.
