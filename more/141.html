Habitat Description

The Gasteropelecus sternicla is found in the slow-moving rivers and streams of the Amazon Basin, including parts of Brazil, Peru, and Guyana. These waters are typically warm, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F), and are often shaded by dense canopy cover. The habitat is characterized by soft, acidic water with a high content of tannins, creating a dark, tea-colored appearance. The substrate is usually sandy or composed of leaf litter, providing plenty of places to hide.

Scientific Facts

This species is a member of the family Gasteropelecidae, known for their unique hatchet-shaped bodies which facilitate a distinctive mode of flight-like jumping above the water's surface. Growing up to 5.5 cm (2.2 inches) in length, it is distinguished by its silver coloration and black lateral stripe. As of the latest data (2024), it is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, with stable populations in its native range. The Common Hatchetfish is an egg scatterer with eggs typically hatching in 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These fish are surface dwellers, spending most of their time near the water's surface. They are best kept in groups of six or more to reduce stress and promote natural behavior. In the wild, they feed on small insects and crustaceans. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and daphnia. A covered aquarium is essential, as their jumping ability can cause them to leap out of the tank.

Interesting Fact

The Common Hatchetfish has evolved pectoral muscles that are proportionally the largest of any fish species. These muscles allow them to flap their pectoral fins rapidly and glide for short distances above the water which is an adaptation to help escape predators in their natural habitat.
