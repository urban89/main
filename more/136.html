Habitat Description

The Congo Tetra (Phenacogrammus interruptus) is native to the slow-moving rivers and streams of the Congo River Basin in Central Africa. These fish thrive in waters that are soft and slightly acidic, often found in areas with dense vegetation and a sandy or muddy substrate. The natural environment is typically shaded by overhanging foliage, providing a dimly lit habitat with plenty of hiding spots and foraging opportunities.

Scientific Facts

Part of the family Alestidae, this species is known for its shimmering scales and flowing fins. Congo Tetras reach a size of up to 8.5 cm (3.3 inches). They are egg scatterers and provide no parental care, with eggs hatching within 6 to 7 days. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating that their population remains stable in the wild.

Life Style Summary

These tetras are social fish that thrive in groups of six or more, displaying more vibrant colors and natural behavior when kept in schools. They are active swimmers and prefer the middle to lower levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and organic detritus. In captivity, they require a varied diet of high-quality flakes, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

Congo Tetras display sexual dimorphism, with males exhibiting more vibrant colors and longer fins than females. During the breeding season, the male’s fins develop an iridescent blue hue, a characteristic that helps attract females.
