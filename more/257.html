Habitat Description

The Trigonostigma espei, commonly known as the Espei Rasbora, is native to the slow-moving streams and rivers of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand and Cambodia. These waters are often stained dark by tannins from decaying plant material, with a soft, slightly acidic quality. The environment is typically shaded by dense vegetation, providing cover and safety from predators. Aquatic plants are abundant, and the substrate consists of fine sand or silt.

Scientific Facts

Espei Rasbora belongs to the family Cyprinidae and is closely related to the Harlequin Rasbora. They are small, peaceful fish, usually reaching about 3-4 cm (1.2-1.6 inches) in length. Characterized by a distinct, dark wedge-shaped marking on their bodies, they are schooling fish that prefer to stay in groups. This species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, though habitat loss due to human activity could impact localized populations.

Life Style Summary

The Espei Rasbora is an active, social fish that thrives in groups of six or more. It spends most of its time in the mid-levels of the water column, actively swimming and exploring. In the wild, they feed on small insects, zooplankton, and plant matter. In captivity, they are best fed a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or bloodworms. They require a well-planted aquarium.

Interesting Fact

The species was named after the ichthyologist Kottelat, who described it in the 1990s. Unlike many other Rasboras, Espei Rasbora exhibits a distinct copper hue, especially when kept in well-maintained aquariums with appropriate water conditions. Their color intensifies with a high-quality diet and suitable water parameters.
