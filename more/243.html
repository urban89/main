Habitat Description

The Uaru amphiacanthoides is native to the blackwater rivers of the Amazon basin, particularly in regions of Brazil and Colombia. These waters are soft, acidic, and rich in tannins, giving them a tea-colored appearance. The fish thrive in densely vegetated environments with submerged roots and fallen logs, where water temperatures range from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

Uaru cichlids belong to the family Cichlidae and are closely related to the South American species of the genus Heros. They can grow up to 25 cm (10 inches) in length and exhibit laterally compressed bodies, which helps them navigate through dense vegetation. The species is currently listed as "Least Concern" in conservation status, meaning it is not under threat in the wild (2024). However, like many Amazonian species, habitat degradation remains a potential risk.

Life Style Summary

Uaru cichlids are social and peaceful, often found in pairs or small groups. In the wild, they feed primarily on plant material, such as algae and fruits that fall into the water, though they will occasionally consume invertebrates. In an aquarium, they require a diet rich in vegetable matter, such as spirulina-based foods, along with occasional protein sources like brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

Known as "Triangle Cichlids" due to their distinct triangular body shape, Uaru cichlids have a unique breeding behavior. When ready to spawn, the pair will clean a flat surface, such as a rock or log, where the female lays eggs. Both parents actively protect and fan the eggs to ensure proper oxygenation, a rare trait among fish species.
