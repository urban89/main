Habitat Description

Crossocheilus oblongus, commonly known as the Siamese Algae Eater, is native to freshwater rivers and streams in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand and the Malay Peninsula. These fish are typically found in fast-flowing waters with rocky substrates, where they graze on algae. They thrive in water temperatures between 24°C and 26°C (75°F to 79°F) with a neutral to slightly acidic pH.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. It is well known for its effectiveness in controlling algae growth in aquariums. Crossocheilus oblongus is not currently under threat in the wild and is listed as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status (2024). They are distinguishable by the black stripe that runs along their bodies, which fades when the fish is stressed or resting.

Life Style Summary

Siamese Algae Eaters are peaceful and tend to be active swimmers. They are social, often seen in small groups in the wild. Their diet primarily consists of algae, biofilm, and plant matter, though they will also consume small invertebrates. In aquariums, they should be fed a varied diet of algae wafers, vegetable-based foods, and occasional protein-rich supplements.

Interesting Fact

One of the few freshwater fish known to feed on black beard algae, a particularly stubborn type of algae that many aquarium keepers struggle to control, the Siamese Algae Eater is highly valued for this unique behavior.
