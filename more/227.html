Habitat Description

The Dwarf Flag Cichlid (Laetacara curviceps) is found in slow-moving waters of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil and Peru. These environments consist of shallow streams, small rivers, and flooded forest areas, often with a sandy or muddy substrate. The water is typically soft and slightly acidic, and the surroundings are densely vegetated.

Scientific Facts

Laetacara curviceps belongs to the family Cichlidae and grows up to 7 cm (2.8 inches) in length. It was first described in 1895. Unlike many other cichlids, this species is relatively peaceful. The Dwarf Flag Cichlid is not currently listed as endangered in its natural habitat (2024), although deforestation in the Amazon region poses a potential threat.

Life Style Summary

Dwarf Flag Cichlids are peaceful and prefer to live in pairs or small groups. They are bottom-dwellers, often seen sifting through the substrate for food. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and organic material. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of quality pellets, live foods, and frozen foods like brine shrimp and bloodworms. These cichlids are known to be attentive parents, with both males and females caring for the eggs and fry.

Interesting Fact

This species displays an unusual parenting behavior: once the eggs hatch, both parents transfer the fry to pits dug in the substrate, guarding them fiercely until they are ready to swim freely.
