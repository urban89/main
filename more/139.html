Habitat Description

The Niger Tetra (Arnoldichthys spilopterus) is found in the slow-moving rivers and streams of West Africa, particularly in the Niger and Ogun river basins. These fish inhabit freshwater environments with soft to moderately hard water and slightly acidic to neutral pH levels. They are often found in areas with dense vegetation, submerged roots, and a sandy or muddy substrate, providing ample cover and food sources.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the family Alestidae and can reach a size of up to 10 cm (4 inches). Known for their distinctive red eyes and silver bodies with a faint lateral line, they are egg scatterers and do not provide parental care. The eggs typically hatch within 24 to 48 hours. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating a stable population in their native habitats.

Life Style Summary

Niger Tetras are social fish that do best in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and prefer the middle to upper levels of the water column. In the wild, their diet consists of small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flakes, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The striking red eye of this species serves a unique purpose—it helps reduce glare from the sun's reflection on the water surface, aiding their vision in murky environments. This adaptation allows them to better spot predators and prey, enhancing their survival in the wild.
