Habitat Description

The Roman Nose Angelfish (Pterophyllum leopoldi) is found in the slow-moving rivers and tributaries of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil and Peru. These waters are warm, soft, and slightly acidic, with dense aquatic vegetation and submerged roots providing shelter. The natural habitat is shaded by overhanging vegetation, which limits light penetration.

Scientific Facts

Pterophyllum leopoldi is the smallest of the three recognized angelfish species, growing up to 10 cm (4 inches) in body length, with fin extensions reaching 15 cm (6 inches). This species is distinguished by its more pronounced forehead, giving it the name "Roman Nose." It is not currently listed as endangered (2024), although habitat loss and deforestation are ongoing concerns in its native region.

Life Style Summary

These angelfish are generally peaceful, but they can be territorial during breeding. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates and organic debris. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flakes, pellets, and live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and bloodworms. They are best kept in groups, and their tall body shape requires a vertically spacious aquarium.

Interesting Fact

Unlike its close relatives, Pterophyllum leopoldi has a more streamlined body, making it well-adapted to moving through narrow spaces between roots and submerged plants in the dense Amazonian waters.
