Habitat Description

The Hyphessobrycon sweglesi is found in the Orinoco River basin, specifically in Colombia and Venezuela. It thrives in slow-moving, blackwater streams and rivers shaded by dense vegetation. These environments feature soft, acidic water rich in tannins, giving them a dark, tea-like appearance. The substrate typically includes leaf litter, wood, and detritus, offering ample hiding spots and a natural food source.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Characidae and reaches up to 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. Males display a bright red color, while females have a softer orange-red hue. Classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN (2024), it shows no significant threat in the wild. These tetras scatter their eggs and provide no parental care, with eggs typically hatching within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

These fish are active, social creatures that thrive in schools of six or more. They coexist peacefully with other small species and are most comfortable in well-planted aquariums with ample swimming space. In the wild, they consume small insects, crustaceans, and plant material, while in captivity, they do well on a varied diet of flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods.

Interesting Fact

Known for their translucent fins that create a "phantom-like" effect, these fish were first described in the 1950s by Samuel Swegles, who lent his name to the species. Males exhibit a distinctive fin-flicking behavior during courtship, using their extended dorsal fins to attract mates or establish territory.
