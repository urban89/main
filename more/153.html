Habitat Description

Found in the slow-moving waters of the Amazon River Basin, particularly in Brazil, Peru, and Bolivia, Metynnis hypsauchen prefers environments with dense vegetation and soft substrates. These waters are typically warm, slightly acidic, and rich in organic material, providing an ideal habitat with ample cover from aquatic plants and submerged roots.

Scientific Facts

A member of the family Characidae, this species is commonly known as the Silver Dollar due to its round, flattened body shape and reflective, silvery appearance, similar to a coin. *Metynnis argenteus* is another species that shares the common name "Silver Dollar" due to its similar characteristics. Both species can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. As of 2024, they are classified as Least Concern, but threats such as habitat loss and water pollution could impact their populations in the future.

Life Style Summary

These fish are schooling species and thrive in groups of five or more. They are active swimmers and occupy the middle to upper layers of the water column. In their natural habitat, they feed primarily on plant material, including leaves, algae, and fruits. In captivity, they require a diet rich in plant-based foods such as spirulina, vegetable flakes, and fresh vegetables like spinach or lettuce.

Interesting Fact

Silver Dollars have a unique ability to rapidly change color depending on their mood or environmental conditions. This adaptation helps them communicate with each other, avoid predators, and adapt to different levels of light in their natural habitat.
