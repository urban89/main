Habitat Description

The Blue Acara (Andinoacara pulcher) is native to the slow-moving rivers, streams, and lakes of Central and northern South America, including Trinidad. These waters are typically soft to moderately hard, with a neutral to slightly acidic pH. The natural habitat is rich in submerged vegetation and offers plenty of hiding spots with sandy or muddy substrates.

Scientific Facts

Andinoacara pulcher belongs to the family Cichlidae and typically grows up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. Males tend to be larger and more vibrant than females. The species is known for its iridescent blue-green scales. It is not listed as endangered (2024), but its natural habitats face challenges from pollution and habitat destruction due to human activity.

Life Style Summary

Blue Acaras are territorial but relatively peaceful compared to other cichlids. They are bottom-dwellers and prefer to live in well-planted environments with ample space to explore. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, plant material, and detritus. In captivity, they accept a wide range of foods, including pellets, flakes, and frozen or live foods like brine shrimp and worms.

Interesting Fact

Blue Acaras are known for their excellent parental care. Both the male and female participate in guarding the eggs and fry, making them one of the more attentive cichlid species when it comes to offspring care.
