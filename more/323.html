Habitat Description

The Least Killifish (Heterandria formosa) is native to the freshwater habitats of the southeastern United States, particularly in Florida, Georgia, and South Carolina. This species thrives in slow-moving waters such as swamps, ditches, ponds, and small streams. Their natural habitat is often heavily vegetated with aquatic plants and has a soft, muddy or sandy substrate. Water conditions are typically warm, ranging from 18-28°C (64-82°F), and slightly acidic to neutral.

Scientific Facts

Least Killifish belong to the family Poeciliidae and are the smallest livebearing fish in the world, with females growing up to 3.5 cm (1.4 inches) and males up to 2 cm (0.8 inches). Despite their name, they are not true killifish but rather belong to a family that includes guppies and mollies. Heterandria formosa is not currently listed as endangered, and they are considered to be of "Least Concern" by conservation status. They give birth to live young rather than laying eggs, with females capable of storing sperm for several months and giving birth multiple times from a single mating.

Life Style Summary

Least Killifish are peaceful and thrive in small groups. They are often seen swimming near the edges of aquatic vegetation in their natural habitat. They feed on small invertebrates, algae, and detritus. In an aquarium, they do well on a diet of micro-pellets, crushed flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods like daphnia or baby brine shrimp. They prefer a well-planted tank with gentle water flow and temperatures between 22-26°C (72-79°F).

Interesting Fact

Despite their small size, Least Killifish are prolific breeders and can adapt to a wide range of water conditions, making them excellent for beginners. They have a unique reproductive strategy, where females can deliver one fry at a time over several days, rather than all at once, which increases the chances of survival for the young in the wild.
