Habitat Description

The Polypterus ornatipinnis, or Ornate Bichir, is native to the slow-moving waters of Central and East Africa, particularly in the Congo River basin. These fish inhabit floodplains, swamps, and shallow, murky rivers with dense vegetation. They prefer areas with soft, sandy or muddy substrates, ample plant cover, and plenty of submerged debris, where they can hide and ambush prey.

Scientific Facts

Ornate Bichirs belong to the family Polypteridae, a group of primitive fish known for their elongated bodies and lobed pectoral fins. They can grow up to 60 cm (24 inches) in length. The species is currently listed as "Least Concern" on the IUCN Red List (2024), but habitat destruction and pollution pose increasing risks. Polypterus ornatipinnis are obligate air-breathers, relying on both gills and lungs to extract oxygen from water and air.

Life Style Summary

Nocturnal predator that hunts by sensing water movements and vibrations. It feeds on smaller fish, crustaceans, and insects. In captivity, it requires a spacious tank with a tightly fitting lid, as it can jump out of the water. The tank should include hiding places, such as caves or dense plants, and a soft substrate. A diet of meaty foods like fish fillets, shrimp, and earthworms is essential for maintaining its health.

Interesting Fact

The Ornate Bichir is often referred to as a "living fossil" due to its primitive features, such as ganoid scales, a spiral valve intestine, and the ability to breathe air. These traits have remained largely unchanged for millions of years, giving scientists valuable insights into the evolution of early fish species.
