Habitat Description

Brittan’s Rasbora (Rasbora brittani) is native to the slow-moving streams and rivers of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand and Malaysia. These environments are typically shaded by dense vegetation, with soft, slightly acidic to neutral water. The substrate in these habitats is often composed of sand, leaf litter, and decaying plant matter, providing a rich and diverse environment for the species.

Scientific Facts

Rasbora brittani belongs to the family Cyprinidae, which encompasses a wide variety of freshwater fish. This species reaches a maximum size of approximately 5 cm (2 inches). It is a peaceful, schooling fish that is not currently listed as endangered in its natural habitat. Brittan’s Rasbora is an egg scatterer, with no parental care provided; the eggs typically hatch within 24 to 36 hours.

Life Style Summary

Brittan’s Rasboras are social and thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers, commonly found in the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, zooplankton, and organic detritus. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Brittan’s Rasbora is known for its striking coloration, featuring a silvery body with a distinctive black spot near the base of the tail fin. This spot is thought to play a role in confusing predators, making it harder for them to single out an individual fish from the school.
