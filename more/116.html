Habitat Description

The Garnet Tetra (Hemigrammus pulcher) is native to the slow-flowing rivers and blackwater streams of the Amazon basin in South America, particularly in Brazil and Colombia. These waters are typically soft, slightly acidic, and rich in tannins from decomposing plant material, which gives the water a tea-like color. They inhabit areas densely populated with submerged vegetation and leaf litter, providing both food and shelter.

Scientific Facts

Garnet Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are distinguished by their bright red body with a subtle, shimmering golden hue. They typically reach about 4 cm (1.6 inches) in length. Like many tetras, they are egg scatterers and provide no parental care to their offspring. As of 2024, this species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating that they are currently not at risk of extinction in their natural habitats.

Life Style Summary

Garnet Tetras are schooling fish that thrive in groups of six or more, displaying more vivid colors and natural behavior in a group setting. They are peaceful and compatible with other small, non-aggressive fish. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In aquariums, they do well on a varied diet that includes flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

This species was first described by the renowned ichthyologist Carl Eigenmann in 1909. They are named "pulcher," meaning "beautiful" in Latin, due to their striking coloration that resembles a glowing red garnet stone under proper lighting.
