Habitat Description

Found in the Amazon and Orinoco River basins in South America, particularly in Brazil, Peru, and Venezuela, Copella arnoldi inhabits shallow, slow-moving waters like small streams and flooded forest areas. These environments are typically clear, soft, and slightly acidic, with sandy substrates or leaf litter, providing ample hiding spots and natural food sources.

Scientific Facts

Part of the family Lebiasinidae, this species reaches about 6 cm (2.4 inches) in length. Notable for its unique reproductive strategy, it leaps out of water to lay eggs on overhanging leaves. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, with populations considered stable in the wild.

Life Style Summary

Splash Tetras are schooling fish that thrive in groups of six or more, staying mostly near the water's surface. In their natural environment, they feed on insects and larvae. In captivity, they do well on a diet that includes flake food, small floating pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia and mosquito larvae.

Interesting Fact

This species is known for its extraordinary breeding method. Males and females jump out of the water to deposit eggs on leaves above the surface. The male then keeps the eggs moist by splashing them with water using his tail until they hatch, ensuring their survival.
