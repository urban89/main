Habitat Description

The Red Wolf Fish (Erythrinus erythrinus) is found in slow-moving or stagnant waters across South America, including the Amazon and Orinoco River basins. It inhabits shallow regions of streams, ponds, and flooded forest areas, where dense vegetation and submerged roots provide ample cover and hunting grounds.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Erythrinidae, this species is a carnivorous predator known for its elongated body and strong jaw muscles. It typically grows up to 25 cm (10 inches) in length. The Red Wolf Fish is a solitary hunter, feeding primarily on smaller fish, insects, and crustaceans. Current assessments indicate it is not endangered in the wild, with a stable population status (2024).

Life Style Summary

Red Wolf Fish is an ambush predator, characterized by its territorial and aggressive behavior. It prefers to remain hidden among vegetation or debris, waiting to strike at passing prey with a sudden burst of speed. In captivity, it requires a spacious tank with plenty of hiding spots and a diet rich in live or frozen foods, such as fish, worms, and shrimp.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many fish, the Red Wolf Fish is capable of breathing atmospheric air thanks to its modified swim bladder, which functions similarly to a lung. This adaptation allows it to survive in low-oxygen environments and even temporarily move between water bodies during seasonal changes or droughts.
