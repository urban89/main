Habitat Description

Rasbora kalochroma, commonly known as the Clown Rasbora, is native to blackwater rivers and peat swamps in Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo. These waters are stained with tannins from decaying vegetation, creating soft, acidic water. The temperature in their natural habitat ranges from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F). The environment is dimly lit, with plenty of submerged plant matter and roots.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, Clown Rasboras can grow up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length. Rasbora kalochroma is known for its striking red-orange coloration with black patches along the body, making it one of the most vibrant species in the genus. The species is currently listed as "Least Concern" in the wild (2024), but habitat destruction, especially the loss of peat swamps, poses a long-term threat.

Life Style Summary

Clown Rasboras are peaceful, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. In their natural habitat, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and organic detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms or daphnia. They are active swimmers that occupy the middle levels of the aquarium, preferring well-planted tanks.

Interesting Fact

The vibrant coloration of Clown Rasboras is believed to play a role in mating and social behaviors, with more intense colors seen during breeding. In their native blackwater environments, their bright colors stand out against the dark, tannin-rich waters, making them both attractive to mates and visible to schooling companions.
