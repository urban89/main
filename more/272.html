Habitat Description

Devario malabaricus is native to the fast-flowing rivers and streams of South Asia, particularly in India and Sri Lanka. These waters are typically clear and well-oxygenated, with rocky substrates and abundant aquatic vegetation. The temperature in their natural habitat ranges from 22°C to 26°C (72°F to 79°F), with slightly acidic to neutral water.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, the Malabar Danio can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length, making it one of the larger danios. This species is distinguished by its vibrant blue and gold stripes running horizontally along the body. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern," with healthy populations in the wild, though habitat degradation could pose a future risk.

Life Style Summary

Malabar Danios are social, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers that thrive in large aquariums with plenty of open swimming space. In the wild, they feed on small insects, larvae, and plankton. In captivity, they do well with a varied diet of flakes, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Malabar Danios are excellent jumpers, a behavior used to escape predators in the wild. This tendency makes it essential to keep a tightly sealed lid on their aquarium to prevent accidental escapes.
