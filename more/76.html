Habitat Description

The Synodontis nigriventris is native to the slow-moving waters of the Congo River Basin in Central Africa. It inhabits shallow areas with dense vegetation and submerged roots, where it can easily hide and find food. The water in these regions is warm, with temperatures ranging from 22-28°C (72-82°F), and typically soft with slightly acidic to neutral pH levels.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Mochokidae, this species grows to a length of about 10 cm (4 inches). The Upside-down Catfish is named for its unique behavior of swimming upside down, which helps it feed on the surface of the water and the undersides of submerged objects. As of 2024, it is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN due to stable populations in the wild. It is an egg scatterer with no parental care, and its eggs hatch in about 5 to 7 days.

Life Style Summary

This species is a nocturnal fish, spending most of the day hiding among plants or rocks and becoming more active at night. It prefers to live in groups of at least five or more to feel secure. In its natural habitat, it feeds on insects, crustaceans, and algae. In captivity, a varied diet of high-quality sinking pellets, live or frozen foods like bloodworms, and vegetable matter is ideal for maintaining its health.

Interesting Fact

The Upside-down Catfish has developed a darker coloration on its belly than its back, which is the reverse of most fish. This adaptation provides camouflage when swimming upside down near the water's surface, helping it evade predators and surprise prey.
