Habitat Description

Rasbora trilineata, commonly known as the Scissortail Rasbora, is native to freshwater rivers, streams, and lakes in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia. These environments feature clear, slow-moving waters with sandy or rocky substrates and are often densely vegetated. Water temperatures in their natural habitat range from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F), with a neutral to slightly acidic pH.

Scientific Facts

Scissortail Rasboras belong to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length, making them one of the larger species of rasboras. Their name comes from the distinctive scissor-like motion of their tail fin when swimming. As of 2024, the species is classified as "Least Concern" in the wild, but habitat loss due to deforestation and water pollution could threaten their populations in the future.

Life Style Summary

Scissortail Rasboras are peaceful, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. In the wild, they primarily feed on insects, small crustaceans, and organic detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or bloodworms. They are active swimmers and prefer large, well-planted aquariums with plenty of open space for swimming.

Interesting Fact

The unique swimming pattern of the Scissortail Rasbora, where they rapidly snap their tail fin together like scissors, is thought to help them navigate through fast-moving waters and may also play a role in their social interactions within a school.
