Habitat Description

The Barred Pencilfish (Nannostomus espei) is native to the slow-moving, densely vegetated waters of the Amazon basin, particularly in Brazil and Peru. These fish are typically found in shallow creeks, flooded forests, and small tributaries with soft, acidic water. The habitat is characterized by low light conditions due to overhanging vegetation and a substrate covered with leaf litter, providing plenty of hiding spots.

Scientific Facts

Nannostomus espei belongs to the family Lebiasinidae, which includes many small and elongated freshwater species. The Barred Pencilfish grows to a maximum length of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). It is easily recognized by its slim body adorned with multiple dark vertical bars along its sides. These fish are peaceful, schooling fish and thrive in groups. They are currently not listed as endangered, though their populations are affected by habitat degradation.

Life Style Summary

Barred Pencilfish are calm and peaceful, preferring to live in small groups of six or more. They are active swimmers and occupy the middle to upper water columns. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates, insects, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like brine shrimp, daphnia, and small insects.

Interesting Fact

Barred Pencilfish are known for their distinctive head-down swimming posture, which is believed to help them blend into their natural environment among vertical plant stems and leaf litter. This behavior, combined with their unique barred pattern, makes them an interesting and visually appealing addition to any planted aquarium.
