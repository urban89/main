Habitat Description

The Burmese Badis (Badis ruber) inhabits the slow-moving, shallow waters of streams and ponds in Myanmar, often found in areas with dense vegetation and a soft substrate. These habitats are characterized by a slightly acidic to neutral pH and moderate water temperatures, typically ranging from 22°C to 26°C (71.6°F to 78.8°F).

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Badidae, the Burmese Badis is a small, vibrant fish known for its striking coloration, which can range from deep red to orange hues. Males exhibit more intense coloration compared to females, especially during breeding. This species typically grows to about 5-6 cm (2-2.4 inches) in length. It is listed as a species of least concern (2024), though habitat degradation could pose future risks.

Life Style Summary

Burmese Badis are relatively shy and prefer to dwell near the bottom of the tank or water body, hiding among dense plants or under rocks. They are carnivorous, feeding on small invertebrates, insects, and larvae in their natural habitat. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of live or frozen foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia. They are best kept in pairs or small groups in a peaceful community tank with plenty of hiding spaces.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many other aquarium fish, the Burmese Badis exhibits unique reproductive behavior. Males create and guard a small territory during breeding, and they become significantly more vibrant to attract females. The male cares for the eggs, protecting them in a secluded spot until they hatch, showcasing an interesting aspect of parental care rarely seen in other small fish species.
