Habitat Description

Devario regina, commonly known as the Queen Danio, is found in fast-flowing streams and rivers of Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand and Myanmar. These habitats are characterized by clear, oxygen-rich waters with a temperature range of 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F) and slightly acidic to neutral water conditions. Dense vegetation and rocky substrates are typical in these environments.

Scientific Facts

Queen Danios belong to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length. They are known for their streamlined bodies with silver and blue iridescent stripes. As of 2024, this species is classified as "Least Concern" in terms of conservation status, with stable wild populations.

Life Style Summary

These fish are highly active and thrive in schools, preferring groups of six or more. In the wild, they feed on small insects, zooplankton, and plant matter. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp. They are fast swimmers and prefer larger tanks with plenty of open space.

Interesting Fact

Queen Danios are known for their impressive leaping abilities. In the wild, this behavior helps them escape predators, and in captivity, it is important to keep their tanks covered to prevent them from jumping out.
