Habitat Description

Balantiocheilus melanopterus, commonly known as the Silver Shark or Bala Shark, is native to freshwater rivers and lakes in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Cambodia, and parts of Indonesia. These waters are clear and fast-flowing, with dense vegetation along the banks. The species thrives in water temperatures ranging from 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F) and prefers a neutral pH of 6.5 to 7.5.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cyprinidae, the Silver Shark can grow up to 35 cm (14 inches) in length. Despite its popularity in the aquarium trade, this species is currently listed as "Endangered" due to habitat loss and overfishing in the wild (2024). Its streamlined body, silver scales, and black-edged fins make it a striking addition to large aquariums, though its size requires substantial space for swimming.

Life Style Summary

Silver Sharks are active, schooling fish that thrive in groups of at least five individuals. In the wild, they feed on insects, small crustaceans, and plant material. In captivity, they require a varied diet that includes high-quality flake food, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms. These fish are known for their high energy levels and fast swimming speeds, and they require a large aquarium with plenty of open space.

Interesting Fact

Despite being called "sharks," Silver Sharks are not related to true sharks. Their name comes from their torpedo-shaped body and triangular dorsal fin, which resemble those of marine sharks. Interestingly, this species was thought to be extinct in the wild until a small population was rediscovered in the 21st century, sparking renewed conservation efforts.
