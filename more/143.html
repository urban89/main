Habitat Description

The Blackwing Hatchetfish (Carnegiella marthae) is found in the slow-moving tributaries and floodplain waters of the Amazon Basin in South America, particularly in Brazil and Venezuela. These fish inhabit soft, acidic waters that are often shaded by dense vegetation. The natural habitat consists of dark, tannin-stained water with a sandy or muddy substrate, providing ample hiding places among floating plants and submerged roots.

Scientific Facts

Part of the family Gasteropelecidae, this species is known for its distinctive "wing-like" pectoral fins and flat, hatchet-shaped body. Blackwing Hatchetfish reach a maximum size of about 3.5 cm (1.4 inches). They are surface-dwelling fish and egg scatterers, offering no parental care, with eggs hatching within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, indicating stable populations in their native range.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful and thrive in schools of six or more, preferring to stay near the water's surface. They are primarily insectivores, feeding on small insects and larvae that fall onto the water. In captivity, they require a diet of high-quality flake foods, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like mosquito larvae and fruit flies to mimic their natural diet.

Interesting Fact

Blackwing Hatchetfish have a unique ability to "fly" out of the water to escape predators. They achieve this by rapidly flapping their large pectoral fins, similar to how a bird flaps its wings, allowing them to glide over short distances and evade threats, a remarkable adaptation for survival in their natural environment.
