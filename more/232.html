Habitat Description

The Cupid Cichlid (Biotodoma cupido) is found in the slow-moving rivers and floodplain lakes of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Brazil, Colombia, and Peru. These waters are typically soft and slightly acidic, with a substrate composed of fine sand and leaf litter. The natural habitat includes plenty of submerged vegetation and root systems that provide hiding spots.

Scientific Facts

Biotodoma cupido belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 10 cm (4 inches) in length. The species is named for the distinct heart-shaped marking that appears on its flanks, resembling the symbol of Cupid. Currently, it is not considered endangered in the wild (2024), but like many Amazonian species, it faces potential threats from deforestation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Cupid Cichlids are peaceful and thrive in well-planted aquariums with plenty of hiding places. They are bottom-dwellers and often sift through the sand in search of food. In their natural environment, they feed on small invertebrates and detritus. In captivity, they should be provided with a varied diet including sinking pellets, live and frozen foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

During breeding, both parents show remarkable levels of care for their offspring, with the female guarding the nest while the male defends the surrounding territory. The fry are often led by the parents through the aquarium or habitat to find food.
