Habitat Description

The Yellow-tail Acei (Pseudotropheus acei) is native to the shallow, rocky shorelines of Lake Malawi in East Africa. It inhabits areas with a mix of sand and rocks, often found near submerged logs and fallen branches. These regions provide both shelter and feeding grounds. The water is clear, with a temperature range of 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a pH between 7.8 and 8.6.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 12 cm (5 inches) in length. Recognized by its blue body and vibrant yellow tail and dorsal fin, it is one of the few mbuna cichlids known to feed primarily on biofilm and algae scraped from submerged wood and rocks. As of 2024, the species is not endangered, but habitat disturbances and pollution could impact its natural populations.

Life Style Summary

Yellow-tail Aceis are social and active, often forming loose groups in the wild. In captivity, they require large aquariums with plenty of swimming space, rocks for territory, and wood surfaces for grazing. They thrive on a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, spirulina, and vegetable matter. Though generally peaceful for mbuna, they can be territorial and should be housed with other non-aggressive cichlids.

Interesting Fact

Unlike many other mbuna, Yellow-tail Aceis have a unique symbiotic relationship with submerged wood, which serves as a vital feeding ground. This connection to both rocky and woody habitats has allowed them to occupy a niche environment, differentiating them from other algae-grazing cichlids in Lake Malawi.
