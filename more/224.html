Habitat Description

The Checkerboard Cichlid (Dicrossus filamentosus) is native to the slow-moving blackwater streams of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Colombia, Venezuela, and Brazil. These waters are typically acidic with a high concentration of tannins, creating a dark, tea-colored environment. The substrate is often covered in leaf litter, providing plenty of hiding spots.

Scientific Facts

Dicrossus filamentosus belongs to the family Cichlidae and typically reaches a size of 5 cm (2 inches). This species is sexually dimorphic, with males exhibiting extended lyre-shaped caudal fins and more vibrant colors compared to females. The species is not currently listed as endangered (2024), but its habitat faces threats from deforestation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Checkerboard Cichlids are peaceful and enjoy living in pairs or small groups in well-planted aquariums. They are bottom dwellers, spending most of their time exploring the substrate and searching for food. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates and organic matter. In captivity, they accept a varied diet, including micro-pellets, frozen foods, and live foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Males of this species are known to perform elaborate courtship displays, where they flare their fins and display their vibrant colors to attract females. These displays are particularly intense during the breeding season.
