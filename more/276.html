Habitat Description

The Zebra Danio (Danio rerio) is native to freshwater streams, rivers, and rice paddies of South Asia, particularly in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nepal. These fish thrive in slow-moving or stagnant waters with a moderate amount of vegetation and sandy or muddy substrates. They are typically found in shallow areas with a consistent flow, where the water temperature ranges between 18-25°C (64-77°F).

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cyprinidae and is widely known for its horizontal blue and silver stripes. They reach an adult size of about 4-5 cm (1.6-2 inches) and are egg scatterers, releasing eggs over vegetation or substrate without further parental involvement. As of 2024, the species is classified as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, due to its adaptability and widespread distribution in its natural range and captivity.

Life Style Summary

Zebra Danios are social, schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of five or more. They are active swimmers, frequently seen darting around the middle and upper levels of their environment. In the wild, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plankton. In captivity, they accept a variety of foods, including flakes, pellets, and live or frozen options like brine shrimp and bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

This species is one of the first vertebrates to have its genome fully sequenced, making it a crucial model organism in scientific research, especially in genetics, developmental biology, and environmental studies.
