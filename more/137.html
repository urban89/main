Habitat Description

The Jellybean Tetra (Ladigesia roloffi) is native to the freshwater streams and small rivers of Sierra Leone and Liberia in West Africa. These fish are typically found in clear, slow-moving waters with dense vegetation and overhanging trees, which provide shaded conditions. The habitat is often characterized by soft, slightly acidic water with ample leaf litter that creates a natural, subdued environment.

Scientific Facts

Ladigesia roloffi belongs to the family Alestidae, a group of small, colorful African tetras. The Jellybean Tetra grows to about 3 cm (1.2 inches) in length. These fish are known for their translucent bodies with a pearlescent sheen and a distinctive spot near the tail fin. They are peaceful, schooling fish that thrive in groups. While not currently classified as endangered, their wild populations are impacted by habitat loss due to deforestation and water pollution.

Life Style Summary

Jellybean Tetras are active and social, preferring to live in schools of six or more. They swim in the mid-water levels, displaying their best colors when kept in planted aquariums with soft lighting. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, algae, and detritus. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Jellybean Tetras are often sought after for their stunning appearance and small size, making them ideal for nano aquariums. Their translucent bodies can change slightly in appearance based on the lighting and water conditions, enhancing their unique shimmering effect that resembles a floating jewel, adding a dynamic and captivating element to any aquarium setting.
