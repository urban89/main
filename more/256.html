Habitat Description

Gyrinocheilus aymonieri, commonly known as the Chinese Algae Eater, is native to freshwater rivers and streams in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia. These habitats are characterized by fast-moving, clear water with rocky substrates and dense vegetation, which provide ample surfaces for algae growth. Water temperatures in their natural environment range from 24°C to 30°C (75°F to 86°F), with a neutral to slightly alkaline pH.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Gyrinocheilidae and can grow up to 28 cm (11 inches) in length. Gyrinocheilus aymonieri is known for its sucker-like mouth, which it uses to scrape algae off rocks and other surfaces. While it is popular in the aquarium trade for algae control, it can become aggressive as it matures. The species is currently listed as "Least Concern" in the wild (2024).

Life Style Summary

Chinese Algae Eaters are territorial fish that become more aggressive as they grow, particularly toward other bottom-dwelling species. In the wild, they feed primarily on algae and biofilm but will also consume small invertebrates. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of algae wafers, vegetables, and protein-rich foods such as bloodworms. These fish prefer aquariums with plenty of hiding spots and surfaces to graze on.

Interesting Fact

Despite their common use in aquariums for algae control, Chinese Algae Eaters tend to eat less algae as they age, often preferring more protein-rich foods. This behavioral shift is something that fishkeepers need to account for when choosing them as part of an algae management strategy.
