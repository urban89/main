Habitat Description

The Mosquito Rasbora (Boraras brigittae) is native to the slow-moving, tea-colored waters of Borneo, Indonesia. These small fish inhabit peat swamps and blackwater streams, where the water is soft, acidic, and stained dark by tannins from decaying plant matter. The natural environment is densely vegetated, providing ample cover and food sources.

Scientific Facts

Boraras brigittae is a member of the family Cyprinidae. It is one of the smallest freshwater fish in the aquarium trade, growing to a maximum size of about 2 cm (0.8 inches). These fish exhibit sexual dimorphism, with males displaying more intense coloration compared to females. Mosquito Rasboras are oviparous, laying eggs that hatch within 24-48 hours. This species is currently not listed as endangered but faces habitat loss due to deforestation and the degradation of peat swamp ecosystems in Borneo.

Life Style Summary

Mosquito Rasboras are peaceful, schooling fish that thrive in groups of at least six. They are active swimmers, often seen exploring the lower and middle levels of the aquarium. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, larvae, and microscopic organisms. In captivity, they do well on a diet of micro-pellets, finely crushed flake food, and small live or frozen foods like brine shrimp and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Despite their diminutive size, Mosquito Rasboras are known for their vibrant red coloration, which stands out even in dimly lit aquariums. This vivid color is more pronounced in males, particularly during the breeding season. The species is often used as a natural mosquito control agent in their native habitat, as they feed on mosquito larvae, which is how they earned their common name.
