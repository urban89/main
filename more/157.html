Habitat Description

The Hujeta Gar, Ctenolucius hujeta, is native to the rivers and streams of Venezuela and Colombia in South America. These fish inhabit clear, fast-flowing waters with a sandy or rocky substrate. Their natural habitat often includes submerged roots and dense aquatic vegetation, providing ample hiding spots and hunting grounds.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Ctenoluciidae and is characterized by its elongated body and pointed snout, resembling a miniature gar. It can grow up to 30 cm (12 inches) in length. As of 2024, it is listed as Least Concern in terms of conservation status, but deforestation and pollution could pose future threats to their natural habitats.

Life Style Summary

Hujeta Gars are predatory and prefer to live in groups of three or more to feel secure. They are active swimmers, mainly occupying the upper levels of the water column. In the wild, they feed on smaller fish and invertebrates. In captivity, they require a varied diet that includes live or frozen foods like small fish, shrimp, and insects.

Interesting Fact

This species is known for its ability to leap out of the water to catch prey, a behavior that helps them hunt insects flying above the water surface. They have been observed jumping as high as 1 meter (3.3 feet).
