Habitat Description

Found along the rocky shores of Lake Malawi, this species inhabits shallow waters with plenty of crevices and caves. These structures provide both shelter and territory. The water in these areas is clear, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F) and a pH of 7.8 to 8.6.

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cichlidae family, Melanochromis johanni displays notable sexual dimorphism. Males are typically dark blue with light blue stripes, while females and juveniles are bright yellow. They can reach up to 8 cm (3 inches) in length. Although not endangered as of 2024, they face potential threats from habitat disturbances and overfishing.

Life Style Summary

This cichlid is territorial and aggressive, especially during breeding. It thrives in rocky aquariums with plenty of hiding spots and areas to establish territories. In the wild, it feeds on small invertebrates, algae, and organic matter. In captivity, it requires a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, vegetables, and occasional live or frozen foods. Best housed with other robust mbuna species to reduce aggression.

Interesting Fact

Males have the ability to change their coloration rapidly, especially when defending territory or courting. This color change serves as a visual signal to rivals and potential mates, helping them communicate and establish dominance without physical conflict.
