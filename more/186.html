Habitat Description

Maylandia callainos is native to the rocky shores of Lake Malawi in East Africa. These fish inhabit shallow, clear waters with a high pH, typically between 7.8 and 8.6. They thrive in environments with plenty of rock formations, which provide essential hiding spots and breeding sites. The water temperature in their habitat ranges from 23°C to 28°C (73°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

This mbuna species belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 12 cm (4.7 inches) in length. Males are often more vibrant in color compared to females. The species exhibits sexual dimorphism, making it easy to differentiate between genders. As of (2024), it is not considered endangered, but their population is at risk due to habitat destruction and overfishing in Lake Malawi.

Life Style Summary

Known for their territorial and aggressive behavior, these fish establish and defend territories within rock formations. In the wild, they feed on algae and small invertebrates found on rocks. In captivity, they thrive on a diet of high-quality cichlid pellets, along with occasional vegetable matter like spirulina and protein-rich foods such as brine shrimp. Providing enough hiding spots in the aquarium helps reduce aggression.

Interesting Fact

This species practices mouthbrooding, where the female carries fertilized eggs and young fry in her mouth for protection until they are ready to swim independently. Additionally, they can slightly change their coloration based on mood or environmental conditions, a trait used for communication within their group.
