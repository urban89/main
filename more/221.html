Habitat Description

The Panda Dwarf Cichlid (Apistogramma nijsseni) is native to the slow-moving, blackwater streams and tributaries in the Peruvian Amazon. These waters are typically shaded by dense overhanging vegetation, resulting in minimal light penetration. The environment is rich in decaying plant matter, creating soft, acidic water with a dark, tannin-stained appearance.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Cichlidae, the Panda Dwarf Cichlid is a small, sexually dimorphic species, with males reaching around 6 cm (2.4 inches) and females being slightly smaller. The species was first described in 1979 and is recognized for its distinctive black-and-white panda-like markings. It is not currently listed as endangered in its natural habitat (2024), but deforestation poses a risk to its population.

Life Style Summary

Panda Dwarf Cichlids are territorial, especially during breeding, and prefer to live in pairs or small harems with one male and several females. They thrive in aquariums with plenty of hiding spots, such as caves and plants. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates and insect larvae. In captivity, they can be fed high-quality pellets, frozen foods, and live foods like brine shrimp or bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

This species practices biparental care, with both the male and female involved in protecting the eggs and fry. During breeding, the female undergoes a striking color change, becoming more vibrant to ward off potential threats while caring for the offspring.
