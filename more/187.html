Habitat Description

Found in the rocky shores of Lake Malawi in East Africa, this species inhabits sediment-rich environments with plenty of hiding places among the rocks. The water is typically hard and alkaline, with temperatures ranging from 24-28°C (75-82°F).

Scientific Facts

Maylandia lombardoi belongs to the family Cichlidae and is known for its notable sexual dimorphism. Males display bright yellow colors, while females are usually blue with dark vertical bars. They can reach a size of up to 13 cm (5 inches). This mbuna species is adapted to grazing on algae. While not currently endangered, habitat loss and overfishing could impact wild populations (2024).

Life Style Summary

These cichlids are territorial and aggressive, especially the males. They are best housed in large aquariums with ample rock formations to create territories and hiding spots. In the wild, they primarily consume algae, while in captivity, they thrive on cichlid pellets, spirulina, and occasional fresh vegetables. It’s important to provide a diet that mimics their natural feeding habits.

Interesting Fact

This species practices mouthbrooding, where females carry eggs and fry in their mouths for protection. During this period, which can last up to three weeks, they do not eat, relying on stored energy reserves.
