Habitat Description

The Eyespot Rasbora (Brevibora dorsiocellata) is native to the slow-moving rivers, streams, and peat swamps of Southeast Asia, particularly in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore. These waters are characterized by their dark, tea-colored appearance due to tannins released from decaying leaves and organic matter. The natural habitat is often densely vegetated, with soft, acidic water and a sandy or muddy substrate.

Scientific Facts

Eyespot Rasboras belong to the family Cyprinidae and are small, schooling fish that grow to a maximum size of about 4 cm (1.6 inches). They are named for the distinctive black spot, resembling an eye, located on their dorsal fin. Brevibora dorsiocellata is currently not considered endangered in nature, but its populations can be affected by habitat loss due to deforestation and water pollution. These fish are egg scatterers, laying their eggs among vegetation, and show no parental care.

Life Style Summary

Eyespot Rasboras are peaceful and thrive in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers, preferring to occupy the middle to upper levels of the water column. In the wild, they feed on small invertebrates, zooplankton, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia and brine shrimp. They prefer a well-planted tank with gentle water flow and soft, slightly acidic water conditions (pH 5.5-7.0).

Interesting Fact

The Eyespot Rasbora is named for the unique black "eyespot" on its dorsal fin, which is believed to confuse predators and reduce the likelihood of attack.
