Habitat Description

The Glowlight Tetra (Hemigrammus erythrozonus) is native to the Essequibo River basin in Guyana, South America. These fish are typically found in clear, slow-moving streams and tributaries with a sandy substrate and abundant vegetation. The water in their natural habitat is usually soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging from 24°C to 28°C (75°F to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

Glowlight Tetras are part of the Characidae family, which encompasses many small, tropical freshwater fish species. They grow to about 4 to 5 cm (1.5 to 2 inches) in length. These tetras are known for their bright, iridescent orange-red stripe running from their nose to the base of the tail. They are egg scatterers with no parental care, and their eggs typically hatch within 24 to 36 hours. As of 2024, Glowlight Tetras are not considered endangered in their natural habitat.

Life Style Summary

They are schooling fish that prefer to live in groups of six or more. They are peaceful by nature and swim actively in the middle to lower regions of the aquarium. In the wild, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant material. In captivity, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods such as bloodworms and daphnia.

Interesting Fact

The name "Glowlight" comes from its bright, fluorescent stripe, which resembles the appearance of a glow stick. This stripe can reflect light even in low-light conditions, making them easy to spot in their natural habitats. This reflective quality is thought to play a role in communication and schooling behavior, helping the fish stay together in dark, murky waters.
