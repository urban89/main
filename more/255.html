Habitat Description

Garra cambodgiensis, commonly referred to as the Cambodian Log Sucker or False Siamese Algae Eater, is native to the fast-flowing rivers and streams of Southeast Asia, particularly in Cambodia and Thailand. These waters are typically shallow, clear, and have a rocky substrate with abundant vegetation. Water temperatures in their natural habitat range from 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F), with slightly acidic to neutral pH levels.

Scientific Facts

This species belongs to the family Cyprinidae and can grow up to 15 cm (6 inches) in length. Garra cambodgiensis is often confused with the Siamese Algae Eater, though it is a distinct species. It plays an important role in its ecosystem by consuming algae and biofilm on submerged surfaces. As of now, the species is not considered at risk and is classified as "Least Concern" (2024).

Life Style Summary

Cambodian Log Suckers are generally peaceful fish but can become territorial with their own kind, especially in smaller spaces. In their natural habitat, they graze on algae, plant matter, and small invertebrates. In aquariums, they should be provided with a varied diet of algae wafers, plant-based foods, and occasional protein-rich supplements such as bloodworms or daphnia.

Interesting Fact

Unlike other algae-eating species, Garra cambodgiensis is known for its ability to thrive in both high-flow environments and quieter areas, making it highly adaptable to different aquarium setups. Despite its peaceful nature, it is known to become more aggressive when food is scarce, so providing sufficient food is essential.
