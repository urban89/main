Habitat Description

The Black Widow Tetra (Gymnocorymbus ternetzi) is native to the slow-flowing rivers, streams, and flooded forests of the Paraguay and Guaporé river basins in South America. These fish are often found in areas with dense vegetation and submerged roots, where the water is soft and slightly acidic. The natural habitat is characterized by dim lighting due to overhanging foliage and a substrate of sand or leaf litter, which creates a dark, tannin-rich environment.

Scientific Facts

This species is part of the Characidae family and is well-known in the aquarium hobby for its unique coloration and hardiness. Black Widow Tetras grow to approximately 2 inches (5 cm) in length. They have a compressed body with a silver base color and distinctive black vertical bands. These fish are egg scatterers and do not exhibit any parental care. The conservation status of this species is currently of "Least Concern," indicating a stable population in the wild (2024).

Life Style Summary

Black Widow Tetras are peaceful, social fish that thrive in groups of six or more. They are active and prefer swimming in the middle levels of the water column. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant matter. In captivity, they do well with a diet of high-quality flakes, micro-pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms or daphnia. These fish enjoy planted tanks with plenty of hiding spots to mimic their natural habitat.

Interesting Fact

The name "Black Widow" comes from the fish's distinct black fins and striking body markings, which are thought to resemble the shape of a black widow spider. Despite their somewhat intimidating name, they are peaceful community fish.
