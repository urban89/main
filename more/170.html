Habitat Description

The Spilo (Champsochromis spilorhynchus) is native to the deep, open waters of Lake Malawi in East Africa. It is often found in the pelagic zones of the lake, where the water is clear, hard, and alkaline. This species prefers areas with little to no cover, where it can roam freely in search of prey.

Scientific Facts

Spilos belong to the family Cichlidae and are one of the larger species in the lake, growing up to 25 cm (10 inches) in length. They are streamlined predators, known for their elongated bodies and powerful jaws, adapted to catch smaller fish. The population of this species remains stable in the wild, with no immediate conservation concerns (2024).

Life Style Summary

Spilos are solitary and highly predatory. They hunt by swiftly darting at smaller fish in the open waters of the lake. In captivity, they require large tanks with open swimming space and should be fed a diet rich in high-protein foods such as live or frozen fish, shrimp, and pellets formulated for large carnivorous cichlids.

Interesting Fact

Spilos have a unique hunting method: they stalk their prey in the vast open waters of Lake Malawi, using their streamlined bodies to accelerate quickly when striking. This hunting strategy mimics the behavior of marine predators, which is unusual for freshwater species.
