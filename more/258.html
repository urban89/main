Habitat Description

The Harlequin Rasbora (Trigonostigma heteromorpha) is found in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore. These fish prefer slow-moving, acidic waters rich in leaf litter and submerged roots, often sheltered by dense vegetation.

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cyprinidae family, this species grows to about 4-5 cm (1.6-2 inches) in length. It is known for its black triangular patch on its body, which helps it blend with its surroundings. They are egg-layers, attaching their eggs to the undersides of leaves. The species is currently categorized as "Least Concern" on the IUCN Red List (2024) due to a stable wild population.

Life Style Summary

This fish is a peaceful, schooling species that thrives in groups of six or more, swimming actively in the middle and upper water levels. Its natural diet includes small insects and zooplankton, but in captivity, it adapts well to flake food, micro-pellets, and live or frozen options like bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

The distinctive black patch on their body serves as both a camouflage mechanism and a social signal, playing a crucial role in mate selection and protection from predators in their native environment.
