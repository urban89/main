Habitat Description

The Diamond Tetra (Moenkhausia pittieri) is native to the freshwater lakes and slow-moving rivers of Venezuela, particularly in the Lake Valencia basin. These fish prefer clear waters with a pH ranging from slightly acidic to neutral and temperatures between 24-28°C (75-82°F). Their natural habitat is often surrounded by dense vegetation, which provides cover and contributes to the slightly tannin-stained water.

Scientific Facts

As a member of the family Characidae, Diamond Tetras are known for their shimmering, silver scales with iridescent reflections, which give them their common name. They typically grow up to 6 cm (2.4 inches) in length. The species is an egg scatterer, and they provide no parental care, with the eggs hatching in approximately 24 to 48 hours. Their current conservation status is "Least Concern," indicating stable populations in the wild as of 2024.

Life Style Summary

Diamond Tetras are peaceful and prefer to be kept in groups of six or more. They are active swimmers, generally occupying the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In their natural environment, they feed on small insects, larvae, and plant material. In captivity, they thrive on a balanced diet of high-quality flake food, pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms or brine shrimp. A planted tank with subdued lighting is ideal to replicate their natural habitat.

Interesting Fact

The iridescence of the Diamond Tetra's scales intensifies as the fish matures, making them more visually striking with age. This increased brilliance is due to the thickening of guanine crystals in their scales.
