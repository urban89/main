Habitat Description

The Long-fin Tetra (Brycinus longipinnis) is native to the slow-moving rivers, lakes, and coastal streams of West Africa, particularly in countries like Nigeria, Cameroon, and Ghana. They are typically found in freshwater environments with soft, slightly acidic to neutral water, where there is dense vegetation and submerged roots that provide shelter and food. The substrate in these habitats is usually composed of sand or fine gravel.

Scientific Facts

Belonging to the family Alestidae, this species can grow up to 12 cm (4.7 inches) in length. They are known for their elongated fins and streamlined body. Long-fin Tetras are egg scatterers that provide no parental care, and their eggs hatch within 36 to 48 hours. As of 2024, they are listed as "Least Concern" by the IUCN, reflecting stable populations in their native habitats with no immediate threats to their survival.

Life Style Summary

These tetras are social fish that do best in groups of six or more, where they display natural schooling behavior. They are active swimmers, occupying the middle to upper levels of the water column. In the wild, they feed on small insects, crustaceans, and plant material. In captivity, they thrive on a varied diet that includes high-quality flake food, small pellets, and occasional live or frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

Long-fin Tetras are known for their speed and agility, which they use to dart quickly between plants and roots to evade predators. Their elongated fins are believed to help them maneuver more effectively in water currents, providing both a functional and aesthetic advantage in their natural environment.
