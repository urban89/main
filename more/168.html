Habitat Description

The Red Fin Hap (Copadichromis borleyi) is native to Lake Malawi in East Africa. It inhabits rocky shorelines and open sandy areas near the shore, where it can swim freely. The water in these regions is clear, hard, and alkaline, providing an ideal environment for this species.

Scientific Facts

Red Fin Haps belong to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 18 cm (7 inches) in length. Males are known for their striking red fins and vibrant blue bodies, while females are typically more subdued in color. This species is a maternal mouthbrooder, with females holding the eggs and fry in their mouths until they are ready to swim freely. As of now, the population of this species remains stable in the wild, with no endangerment concerns (2024).

Life Style Summary

Red Fin Haps are relatively peaceful cichlids but can be territorial during breeding. They prefer open spaces to swim and rock structures for hiding and breeding. In the wild, they feed on plankton, while in captivity they thrive on high-quality cichlid pellets, supplemented with vegetable matter and occasional live or frozen food.

Interesting Fact

Male Red Fin Haps build small sand "pits" to attract females for breeding. The male's vibrant colors become even more intense during this time, as he uses both visual and behavioral displays to court potential mates.
