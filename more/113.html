Habitat Description

The Ember Tetra (Hyphessobrycon amandae) is native to the slow-moving streams and rivers of the Araguaia River basin in central Brazil. These waters are typically warm, clear, and densely vegetated, providing ample cover for these small, schooling fish. The natural habitat is rich in plant life, which helps to maintain the slightly acidic and soft water conditions that Ember Tetras prefer. The shaded environments created by overhanging trees reduce light penetration, mimicking the subdued lighting conditions that these fish are accustomed to in the wild.

Scientific Facts

Ember Tetras belong to the family Characidae, a diverse group of freshwater fish known for their vibrant colors and peaceful nature. They typically grow to about 2 cm (0.8 inches) in length, making them one of the smaller tetra species. Their bright orange-red coloration is particularly striking against a backdrop of green plants. Ember Tetras are egg scatterers, and like many tetras, they do not exhibit parental care. The eggs generally hatch within 24 to 36 hours, depending on the water temperature.

Life Style Summary

Ember Tetras are peaceful, schooling fish that thrive in groups of at least six or more. They are active swimmers and prefer the middle to upper levels of the aquarium. In their natural habitat, they feed on small invertebrates, zooplankton, and plant matter. In an aquarium setting, they do well on a diet of high-quality flake food, micro-pellets, and the occasional treat of live or frozen foods like brine shrimp or daphnia. The water temperature in their habitat typically ranges between 24-28°C (75-82°F).

Interesting Fact

Ember Tetras are known for their ability to bring a vibrant splash of color to any aquarium, despite their small size. Their brilliant orange hue becomes even more intense when they are kept in a well-planted tank with dark substrate. Interestingly, the Ember Tetra was named after Heiko Bleher's mother, Amanda Bleher, who discovered the species in the wild.
