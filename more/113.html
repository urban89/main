Habitat Description

The Ember Tetra (Hyphessobrycon amandae) is native to the slow-moving waters of the Araguaia River basin in Brazil. These waters are typically warm, soft, and slightly acidic. The natural environment includes dense vegetation and leaf litter on the substrate, providing the fish with plenty of hiding spots and a dimly lit, tannin-rich habitat that enhances their vibrant coloration.

Scientific Facts

Ember Tetras belong to the family Characidae and are small, schooling fish, usually reaching a size of about 2 cm (0.8 inches). The species was named in honor of Amanda Bleher ("amandae", the mother of renowned aquarist Heiko Bleher, who discovered them. Ember Tetras are egg scatterers, and like many other tetras, they do not exhibit parental care. Their eggs typically hatch within 24 to 36 hours. The species is not currently listed as endangered, but its natural habitat is under threat due to deforestation and habitat degradation.

Life Style Summary

Ember Tetras are peaceful and highly social fish that thrive in groups. They are active swimmers, spending most of their time in the middle to lower levels of the aquarium. In the wild, their diet consists of small invertebrates, algae, and organic detritus. In captivity, they are best fed with high-quality micro-pellets, crushed flakes, and occasional live or frozen foods such as daphnia or cyclops. They prefer water temperatures between 23-28°C (73-82°F) and are most comfortable in a well-planted aquarium with soft lighting.

Interesting Fact

Ember Tetras are known for their stunning orange-red coloration, which intensifies in well-maintained, densely planted aquariums. Despite their small size, they can significantly enhance the visual appeal of a tank, especially when kept in larger groups. Their ease of care and breeding also makes them a favorite among aquarists, particularly those interested in nano aquariums.
