Habitat Description

The Gold Saum (Andinoacara rivulatus), also known as the Green Terror, is native to the freshwater rivers and streams of Ecuador and northern Peru. These waters are generally slow-moving, with sandy or muddy substrates and abundant submerged vegetation. The natural habitat provides plenty of hiding spots with soft, slightly acidic to neutral water conditions.

Scientific Facts

Andinoacara rivulatus belongs to the family Cichlidae and can grow up to 30 cm (12 inches) in length. Males tend to be larger and develop a noticeable hump on their heads. The species was first described in 1860. It is not currently considered endangered (2024), although its natural habitat faces challenges from pollution and habitat degradation.

Life Style Summary

Gold Saums are territorial and somewhat aggressive, especially during breeding. They thrive in aquariums with ample space and hiding spots. In the wild, they feed on small fish, insects, and invertebrates. In captivity, they can be fed high-quality pellets, frozen foods, and live foods such as earthworms and crickets. Breeding pairs can be fiercely protective of their young.

Interesting Fact

During territorial displays and breeding, males of this species can undergo dramatic color changes, becoming intensely vibrant with iridescent green and blue hues, making them a striking sight in their natural environment.
