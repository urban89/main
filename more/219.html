Habitat Description

Native to the shallow waters of Paraguay and Argentina, this species is commonly found in slow-moving creeks, ponds, and small rivers. The environment is typically dense with aquatic vegetation and a sandy or muddy substrate. Water conditions are usually soft and slightly acidic, with temperatures ranging from 23 to 28°C (73 to 82°F).

Scientific Facts

A member of the Cichlidae family, this dwarf cichlid species is known for its colorful appearance and peaceful demeanor. Males can grow up to 7 cm (2.8 inches) in length, while females are slightly smaller. The species is not currently threatened in its natural habitat, but local populations can be affected by environmental changes and water pollution (2024).

Life Style Summary

Generally peaceful, this fish prefers to live in pairs or small groups and thrives in well-planted aquariums with plenty of hiding spots. It exhibits strong parental care, with both parents tending to the eggs and fry. In its natural habitat, it feeds on small invertebrates and plant matter, while in captivity, it accepts a variety of foods including high-quality flakes, pellets, and live or frozen foods such as daphnia or bloodworms.

Interesting Fact

This species has a remarkable ability to adapt to varying water conditions, making it a popular choice for aquarists. The males can change their coloration to become more vibrant during the breeding season, using their colors to attract females and establish dominance over territories.
