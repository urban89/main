Habitat Description

Native to the slow-moving waters of the Amazon Basin, particularly in Peru, Colombia, and Brazil, Nannostomus unifasciatus inhabits shallow streams and flooded forest areas. These environments are typically rich in tannins, creating a dark, acidic water condition with plenty of leaf litter and submerged plant roots, providing cover and feeding grounds.

Scientific Facts

A member of the family Lebiasinidae, this fish is known for its elongated body and distinctive single dark line running along its side from head to tail. It typically grows to a maximum length of about 6 cm (2.4 inches). As of 2024, it is classified as Least Concern regarding conservation status, but ongoing deforestation and water pollution could threaten its habitat in the future.

Life Style Summary

These fish are peaceful and thrive best in groups, often seen swimming at a slight angle. They prefer the upper water levels in the aquarium and are active throughout the day. In the wild, their diet consists mainly of small insects and micro-organisms. In aquariums, they should be fed a variety of small foods, including micro-pellets, high-quality flakes, and live or frozen foods like brine shrimp.

Interesting Fact

This species is known for its unique reproductive behavior. Unlike many other fish, it lays its eggs on plant leaves and roots, where they adhere until hatching. This method provides protection from predators and ensures better oxygen flow to the developing eggs.
